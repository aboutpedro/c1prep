The passive and causative

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

The passive puts the focus on what happens rather than who does it. At C1 you need it in every form – with modals, infinitives and -ing forms – and in the formal reporting structures common in news and academic writing.

Passive forms

formexample
present / past continuousThe road is being repaired. The bridge was being built.
perfectHe hasn’t been seen since Tuesday. The tickets had been sold.
modalIt must be signed. It should have been dealt with years ago.
infinitiveShe was the first woman to be appointed. He claims to have been misled.
-ingI hate being told what to do. He avoided being hit.

Careful: need + -ing already has a passive meaning: The windows need cleaning = need to be cleaned.

It is said that… / He is said to…

With verbs like say, think, believe, expect, report, understand, know there are two patterns.

It is + past participle + thatIt is said that the castle is haunted.
subject + is + past participle + to-infinitiveThe castle is said to be haunted.
  • He is said to be living in Argentina.happening now
  • The fire is thought to have started in the kitchen.earlier event: perfect infinitive
  • The painting is believed to have been stolen.earlier + passive
  • The company is understood to have been planning cuts for months.

have and get something done

have / get + object + past participlesomeone does it for you: I had my hair cut. We’re getting the kitchen repainted.
the same form, something badShe had her bag stolen.
have + person + verbThey had the builders knock down the wall.
get + person + to-infinitiveMy boss got me to redo it.
get + object + -ingI finally got the printer working.

Careful: make has no to in the active but needs it in the passive: They made him pay → He was made to pay.
