Cambridge English exams, A2 to C2

Checked against Cambridge English’s own pages on 26 November 2026

There is one exam for each level from A2 to C2. All five test reading, writing, listening and speaking, and they all report your result on the same scale. Choose an exam for its guide: every paper and part, the timings, and how the score works.

The exams used to be known by their initials, and many people still use them: KET, PET, FCE, CAE and CPE.

One scale for every exam

Your result is a score on the Cambridge English Scale. The same score means the same level whichever exam you take: 180, for example, is grade A in B2 First, grade C in C1 Advanced and a C1 certificate in C2 Proficiency, and it always means C1. Each exam reports scores a little above and below its own level, so a strong result can show the level above, and a weaker one can still earn a certificate for the level below.

Every exam gives you a score for each skill as well as an overall score, which is their average. Each guide explains how the papers add up.

For Schools, on a computer, on paper

  • For Schools. A2 Key, B1 Preliminary and B2 First also come in a version for school-age learners, with the same format and topics for younger people.
  • On a computer or on paper. The papers and parts are the same either way. Paper-based A2 Key ends in 2026: from 2027, A2 Key is taken on a computer only, while A2 Key for Schools is still available on paper.
  • Listening on the computer. Since 1 November 2025, the Listening multiple-matching part (B2 First Part 3, C1 Advanced and C2 Proficiency Part 4) is drag and drop.

Which exam is for you?

Choose the exam for the level you are at, or will be at by the exam date. Not sure? Take the level test (about 15 minutes), or read what each level means on the CEFR levels page.
