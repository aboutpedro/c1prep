Future forms and the future in the past

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

English has many ways of talking about the future. At C1 you need the forms that add a time perspective (will be doing, will have done), the formal and fixed ones (be to, be bound to, be on the point of) and the future seen from the past (was going to, would).

Future continuous and future perfect

formuseexample
will be + -ingin progress at a future time; polite questions about plansThis time tomorrow I’ll be lying on a beach. Will you be going past the shop?
will have + past participlefinished before a future timeBy Friday I’ll have finished the report.
will have been + -ingduration up to a future timeIn June she’ll have been working here for twenty years.

Careful: after when, as soon as, until, before, once use a present tense, not will: I’ll call you as soon as I get the results. Once you have finished, hand it in.

Fixed future expressions

be about tovery soon: The train is about to leave.
be on the point of + -ingvery soon: I was on the point of giving up.
be due toscheduled: The flight is due to land at 6.40.
be bound tocertain: You’re bound to pass.
be toofficial plans and instructions: The President is to visit Brazil. Passengers are to show their passports.

Careful: If we are to reach the summit… means “if we want to”, not a plan.

The future in the past

To talk about what was in the future at a past moment, move the future form one step back.

  • I was going to call you, but I fell asleep.a plan that didn’t happen
  • She knew she would become a doctor.will → would
  • We were about to leave when it started to snow.
  • He was to become a famous painter.what really happened later
  • We were to have spent the summer in Greece.a plan that never happened (formal)
