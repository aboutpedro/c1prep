Inversion and emphasis

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

To make writing more emphatic or dramatic, C1 English moves words to the front of the sentence. After a negative or restrictive word at the start, the subject and auxiliary swap places, just like in a question.

Negative adverbials + inversion

adverbialexample
Never (before), Rarely, SeldomNever have I seen such a sunset. Seldom does a first novel receive such praise.
No sooner… than / Hardly… whenNo sooner had he arrived than he was asked to leave. Hardly had we sat down when the alarm went off.
Not only… but alsoNot only did she win, but she also broke the record.
Under no circumstances, On no account, In no wayUnder no circumstances should passengers open the doors.
LittleLittle did they know that they were being watched.
Not until / Only when / Only after / Only laterNot until the next morning did we realise. Only when you have finished may you leave.

Careful: with Not until… and Only when / after…, the inversion comes in the main clause, not straight after the time phrase: Only when I got home did I realise.

so and such at the front

  • So strong was the wind that trees were uprooted.So + adjective + be + subject + that
  • Such was the demand that extra trains were added.Such + be + subject + that
  • Strange as it may seem, I enjoy exams.= although it may seem strange
  • Try as she might, she couldn’t open it.= however hard she tried

Cleft sentences and emphatic do

What… is / was…What I need is a holiday. What she did was book the flights.
All… is / was…All I did was ask a question.
It is / was… that / whoIt was my sister who told me. It was not until midnight that they arrived.
The reason why / The place whereThe reason why I’m calling is…
do / does / did + verbI did tell you! She does talk a lot.
