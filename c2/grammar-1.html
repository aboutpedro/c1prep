Grammar

Perfect and continuous forms

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

At C2, the question is rarely which tense but which aspect: is the action complete (perfect), in progress (continuous), or both? Perfect forms also appear in infinitives and participles.

Perfect forms beyond the tenses

formexample
perfect infinitiveShe claims to have met the president. He seems to have forgotten.
perfect continuous infinitiveHe appears to have been working all night.
perfect participleHaving finished the report, she went home.
perfect gerundHe denied having taken the money.
modal + perfectYou should have told me. It must have rained.

Simple or continuous?

  • I’ve read three chapters. / I’ve been reading your book.result and number vs. unfinished activity
  • I see what you mean. / You’re being very difficult today.state vs. temporary behaviour
  • I could tell someone had been using my computer.evidence of an earlier activity

Time frames to watch

  • It was the best meal I had ever had.up to a point in the past
  • This is the third time she has lost her keys.This is the first / second… time + present perfect
  • By June, they will have been married for fifty years.up to a point in the future
  • Once you have completed the form, hand it in.time clauses: present perfect for a future action completed first
