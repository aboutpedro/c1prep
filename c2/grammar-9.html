Grammar

Wishes, preferences and the subjunctive

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

English uses past forms for situations that are not real: wishes, preferences, and hypotheses. Formal English also keeps a few subjunctive forms.

Wishes and regrets

a present wishI wish I had a car. If only I could swim.
a past regretI wish I had known. If only I hadn’t said that.
annoyance / wanting changeI wish you would stop interrupting.

Unreal past after other expressions

  • I’d rather you didn’t smoke. I’d sooner you told me now.
  • It’s (high / about) time we made a decision.
  • He talks as if he were the boss. Suppose someone offered you a million…
  • I’d rather stay in. I’d prefer to stay in.same subject: no past

The subjunctive

After verbs and adjectives of importance and demand, formal English uses the base form for every person, even he, she and it.

  • The doctor recommended that she take more rest.not takes
  • It is essential that every student complete the form. They demanded that it be published.
  • It is vital that he not be told.negative: not + base form
  • Be that as it may · Come what may · Suffice it to sayfixed expressions

British English often uses should instead: recommended that she should take more rest.
