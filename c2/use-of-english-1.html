Reading and Use of English · Part 1

Multiple-choice cloze

For questions 1–8, read the text below and decide which answer (A, B, C or D) best fits each gap. There is an example at the beginning (0).

Each gap tests which word goes with the words around it: collocations (pay attention), fixed phrases, phrasal verbs, the preposition or pattern that follows a word, and words with similar meanings that are used differently.

How to do it

  1. Read the whole text quickly first, without stopping at the gaps, so you know what it is about.
  2. At each gap, look at the words on both sides. What comes after the gap is often the key: a preposition, to + verb, that, a noun.
  3. Try all four options in the sentence. Cross out the ones that are impossible because of grammar (the wrong pattern after them).
  4. Of the options left, choose the one that sounds natural with these exact words, not just the one with the right meaning.
  5. Read the sentence again with your answer in it. Never leave a gap empty: there is no penalty for a wrong answer.

Watch out for

  • Options with almost the same meaning: raise / rise / arise, affect / effect. Only one fits the grammar and the collocation.
  • A word that is right in your language but not in English. Trust the phrase you have seen in English texts.
  • The preposition after the gap: in charge of, with regard to, at the expense of.

In the C2 Proficiency exam, the whole Reading and Use of English paper takes 90 minutes. Aim for about 6 minutes on this part.
