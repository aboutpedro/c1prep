Reading and Use of English · Part 2

Open cloze

For questions 9–16, read the text below and think of the word which best fits each gap. Use only one word in each gap. There is an example at the beginning (0).

The gaps test grammar words: prepositions, articles, pronouns, auxiliaries, linking words, relative pronouns, parts of fixed phrases and phrasal verbs. There are no options, so you write one word.

How to do it

  1. Read the whole text first for the general meaning.
  2. At each gap, decide what kind of word is missing: a preposition? a verb (which form)? a linker? Read the whole sentence, not only the words next to the gap.
  3. Check the meaning across sentences: contrast (however, though), reference back (which, this, such), comparison (as, than).
  4. Look for fixed phrases: by no means, in spite of, as long as, for the sake of.
  5. Read the finished text again. Every answer must make the sentence grammatical and keep the meaning of the text.

Watch out for

  • One word only: a lot or in spite is two words. Contractions such as don’t are not accepted.
  • Spelling counts: a misspelt word is wrong.
  • Negatives and contrasts: a missing not, no or although can turn the meaning upside down.
  • Don’t stop at the first word that fits the gap: check it still fits once you read to the end of the sentence.

In the C2 Proficiency exam, the whole Reading and Use of English paper takes 90 minutes. Aim for about 8 minutes on this part.
