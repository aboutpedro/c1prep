Tenses and aspect

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

At C1 the question is rarely “which tense?” and more often “which aspect?”: simple for completed facts and states, continuous for activities in progress, perfect to link two times. The same choices run through present, past and future.

Present perfect: simple or continuous?

useformexample
a result you can count or measuresimpleI’ve read it three times. Prices have risen by 12%.
an activity over a period, often with a visible effectcontinuousI’ve been fixing the bike all morning (look at my hands).
a situation still going oncontinuous (or simple with state verbs)We’ve been waiting for an hour. I’ve known her for years.

Careful: after This is the first / second time… use the present perfect simple: This is the third time you’ve asked.

Telling a story

  • I was driving home when I realised I’d left the oven on.background + event
  • By the time they reached us, we had been trapped for two days.earlier state up to a past moment
  • She was out of breath because she had been running.earlier activity that explains a past situation
  • It was the first time he had performed in public.past perfect after It was the first time

State verbs used as actions

Verbs like think, have, see, taste, weigh, be describe states, so they are usually simple. When they describe an action, they can be continuous.

stateaction
I think it’s a mistake. (opinion)I’m thinking about moving. (considering)
The soup tastes great.The chef is tasting the sauce.
He is unreasonable. (his character)He’s being unreasonable. (today’s behaviour)
I see what you mean.I’m seeing the manager tomorrow. (meeting)

Habits

would + verbrepeated past actions (not states): Grandad would take us fishing.
used to + verbpast habits and states: I used to live in Lisbon.
will + verbtypical present behaviour: He’ll sit there for hours.
keep + -ing / be always + -ingannoying habits: He keeps forgetting his keys. She’s always complaining.

Careful: be / get used to + -ing means “be accustomed to”, not a past habit: I’m not used to getting up this early.
