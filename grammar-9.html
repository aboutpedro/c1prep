Grammar

Verb patterns

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

Most verbs are followed by either an -ing form or a to-infinitive, and you just have to learn which. A few can take both – with a change in meaning – and at C1 you also need the perfect and passive forms.

Both forms, different meanings

verb+ -ing / + to-infinitive
remember / forget+ -ing: a memory of something done: I’ll never forget seeing it.
+ to: an action still to do: Remember to lock the door.
stop+ -ing: end an activity: He stopped smoking.
+ to: pause in order to: We stopped to have a coffee.
try+ -ing: as an experiment: Try adding lemon.
+ to: make an effort: I tried to open it.
regret+ -ing: be sorry about the past: I regret speaking like that.
+ to: formal bad news: We regret to inform you…
go on+ -ing: continue: He went on talking.
+ to: do next: She went on to do a PhD.
mean+ -ing: involve: It would mean moving.
+ to: intend: I didn’t mean to upset you.

Perfect and passive forms

to have + past participlean earlier action: She seems to have forgotten.
to be + past participlepassive: The report is expected to be published.
having + past participlean earlier action: He denied having stolen it.
being + past participlepassive: I can’t stand being kept waiting.
to have been + -ingan activity over a period: They appear to have been working on it for weeks.

make, let, help and fixed expressions

  • They made me practise. → I was made to practise.no to in the active, to in the passive
  • They won’t let us take photos. → We aren’t allowed to take photos.
  • It’s worth visiting. There’s no point (in) arguing. It’s no use complaining.
  • I can’t help wondering. I’m looking forward to hearing from you.to is a preposition here
