Grammar

Substitution and ellipsis

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

Good English avoids repeating words. We replace them (so, do so, one, that of) or leave them out when the meaning is clear. These forms often appear in the open cloze.

so and not for a whole clause

  • Is it open? – I think so. / I don’t think so.
  • Will it rain? – I hope not. / I’m afraid so.
  • If so, we should leave now.= if that is true
  • I doubt it.not I doubt so

so / neither + auxiliary; do so

  • I love jazz. – So do I. / I can’t swim. – Neither can I.
  • She’s been to Japan, and so has her husband.the auxiliary matches the first verb
  • They promised to cut prices but have not done so.do so = do that action (formal)

one, ones, that of, those who

  • Have you got a darker one? I need bigger ones.
  • The climate here is milder than that of the north.that of / those of in comparisons
  • Those who arrive late will not be admitted.

Leaving words out

  • Would you like to come? – I’d love to.keep to
  • She speaks Spanish better than I do.
  • They were supposed to call, but they didn’t.
