Grammar

Present simple and present continuous

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

Both tenses talk about the present, but in different ways. The present simple is for things that are generally true: habits, routines and facts. The present continuous is for things that are happening now, or around now.

Present simple

I / you / we / theyhe / she / it
+I workshe works
−I don’t workshe doesn’t work
?Do you work?Does she work?

With he, she and it, the verb gets an -s: work → works, watch → watches, go → goes, study → studies, have → has. After doesn’t and does, the -s disappears: she doesn’t work, not she doesn’t works.

We use it for habits and routines, for facts, and for things that are true for a long time.

The basics

  • I get up at seven every day.
  • My sister works in a hospital.
  • Water boils at 100 degrees.
  • Do you like coffee? – Yes, I do.

You might also see

  • He doesn’t usually drive to work. He takes the bus.Words like always, usually, often, sometimes and never go before the main verb, but after be: She’s always late.
  • How often does your brother call you?
  • The train leaves at 8.15 tomorrow.Timetables use the present simple, even for the future.

Present continuous

Form: am / is / are + -ing.

+I’m working. She’s working. They’re working.
−I’m not working. She isn’t working. They aren’t working.
?Am I working? Is she working? Are they working?

We use it for actions in progress now, at the moment of speaking, and for temporary situations around now. Watch the spelling: make → making, swim → swimming, lie → lying.

The basics

  • Listen! The baby is crying.
  • I’m cooking dinner at the moment.
  • Are you watching this? – No, I’m not.

You might also see

  • I’m reading a great book this week.Not at this exact second, but around now.
  • My cousin is staying with us until she finds a flat.A temporary situation.
  • Prices are going up every month.A situation that is changing.

Which one?

Ask yourself: is this a habit or something always true, or is it happening now?

  • I work in a bank. My job, in general.
  • I’m working from home today. Just today.
  • She plays tennis on Saturdays. / She’s playing tennis right now.

Verbs that don’t usually go in the continuous

Some verbs describe states, not actions, so we normally use them in the present simple, even when we mean “now”: like, love, hate, want, need, know, understand, believe, remember, mean, belong, seem.

  • I know the answer. Not: I’m knowing the answer.
  • Do you understand what I mean?

Careful: a few verbs can be a state or an action. I have two brothers (a state) but I’m having lunch (an action). I think it’s a good idea (an opinion) but I’m thinking about it (the process of thinking).
