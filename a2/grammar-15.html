Grammar

Linking words, time clauses and if

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

Linking words join ideas so that your English sounds connected, not like a list of short sentences: I was tired, so I went to bed. Some of them also need a particular tense after them.

and, but, or, so, because

andadds something: I bought bread and cheese.
buta different or opposite idea: It’s small, but it’s nice.
ora choice: Tea or coffee?
soa result: I was tired, so I went to bed.
becausea reason: I went to bed because I was tired.

when, before, after, until, while

  • Call me when you arrive.
  • I have a shower before I go to bed.

Talking about the future

After when, before, after, until and as soon as, use the present simple, even when you mean the future. The other part of the sentence has will, going to or an instruction.

  • I’ll call you when I get home.Not: when I will get home.
  • Don’t go out until the rain stops.

if + present, will

For a situation that is possible in the future, use if + present simple, and will + verb for the result.

The basics

  • If it rains tomorrow, we’ll stay at home.
  • You’ll pass the exam if you study.The if part can come first or second. Use a comma when it comes first.
  • If we don’t leave now, we’ll miss the bus.

You might also see

  • If you’re hungry, I’ll make you a sandwich.an offer
  • If you heat ice, it melts.always true: present + present
  • If you see Tom, tell him to call me.an instruction

Careful: no will after if: If it rains, not If it will rain.
