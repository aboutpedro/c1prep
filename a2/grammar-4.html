Talking about the future

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

English doesn’t have just one future tense. The three most common ways to talk about the future are going to, will and the present continuous. The one you choose depends on when you made the decision and how sure you are.

going to: plans and things you can see coming

Form: am / is / are + going to + verb. Use it for plans and intentions you made before speaking, and for predictions based on what you can see now.

The basics

  • I’m going to visit my grandparents this weekend.
  • We’re not going to buy a new car this year.
  • Are you going to watch the match?
  • Look at those clouds. It’s going to rain.

You might also see

  • What are you going to do after the course?
  • Be careful! You’re going to fall!You can see it’s about to happen.

will and won’t: quick decisions, offers and opinions

Form: will (’ll) / won’t + verb, the same for everyone. Use it for decisions you make at the moment of speaking, for offers and promises, and for predictions that are just your opinion.

The basics

  • “It’s cold in here.” “I’ll close the window.”
  • Don’t worry, I won’t tell anyone.
  • I think it’ll be sunny tomorrow.

You might also see

  • Will you help me with these bags, please?A request.
  • I don’t think she’ll come.More natural than “I think she won’t come”.
  • He’ll probably be late. / He probably won’t come.Probably goes after will but before won’t.

Present continuous: fixed arrangements

When a plan is already organised, with a time and place and often other people, we usually use the present continuous. Think of things you’d write in a diary or calendar.

  • I’m meeting Sara at six.
  • We’re flying to Lisbon on Friday.
  • What are you doing tonight?
