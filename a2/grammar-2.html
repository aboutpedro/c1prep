Grammar

Past simple and past continuous

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

We use the past simple for finished actions and situations in the past. The past continuous describes an action that was in progress at a past moment. They often work together when we tell a story.

Past simple

all personsbe
+I worked. She went.I / he was · we / they were
−I didn’t work. She didn’t go.wasn’t · weren’t
?Did you work? Did she go?Was it…? · Were you…?

Regular verbs add -ed. Watch the spelling: like → liked, stop → stopped, study → studied, but play → played. Many common verbs are irregular and you just need to learn them: go → went, have → had, see → saw, buy → bought, make → made, take → took, come → came, get → got, eat → ate, write → wrote.

The basics

  • I watched a film last night.
  • We went to the beach on Sunday.
  • I didn’t see him at the party.
  • Did you enjoy the concert? – Yes, I did.

You might also see

  • What did you do at the weekend?
  • Who told you that?When who is the subject, there’s no did.
  • When I was a child, I walked to school every day.The past simple can also describe past habits.
  • We were tired, so we didn’t go out.

Careful: after did and didn’t, use the base verb: I didn’t go, not I didn’t went; Did you see it?, not Did you saw it?

Past continuous

Form: was / were + -ing.

+I was sleeping. They were talking.
−She wasn’t listening. We weren’t waiting.
?Was it raining? What were you doing?

We use it for an action that was in progress at a particular time in the past. It started before that moment and continued after it.

The basics

  • At eight o’clock I was having dinner.
  • What were you doing at ten last night?
  • They weren’t listening to the teacher.

You might also see

  • The sun was shining and people were sitting outside.Setting the scene at the start of a story.
  • While I was cooking, my brother was doing his homework.Two actions in progress at the same time.

Using them together

The past continuous is the longer, background action. The past simple is the short action that happens in the middle of it. We often join them with when and while.

  • I was walking home when it started to rain.
  • While she was sleeping, someone knocked at the door.
  • I broke my arm when I was playing football.

You might also see

  • When the phone rang, I answered it.One action after the other, so both are in the past simple.
  • When Tom arrived, we were eating. / When Tom arrived, we ate.In the first, we were already eating. In the second, we started eating after he arrived.
