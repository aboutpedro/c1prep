Countable and uncountable nouns

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

Some nouns are countable: you can count them (one apple, two apples). Others are uncountable: you can’t count them, so they have no plural (water, money, information). This changes the words we use with them.

Countable or uncountable?

countableuncountable
a chair, two chairsfurniture (no a, no plural)
an egg, three eggsbread, rice, milk, cheese
a coin, some coinsmoney
a suitcase, two suitcasesluggage

Some nouns are uncountable in English even if they’re countable in your language: information, advice, news, homework, furniture, luggage, weather, traffic. Uncountable nouns take a singular verb.

  • The news is good. Not: The news are good.
  • Can you give me some advice? Not: an advice / some advices.

To count them, use a word like piece, bottle, cup, slice, kilo:

  • a piece of advice · a bottle of water · a cup of tea · two slices of bread

You might also see

  • I’d like a coffee, please.In cafés, a coffee means a cup of coffee.
  • I don’t have time. / We had a great time.Time = minutes and hours; a time = an experience.

some and any

Use some in positive sentences and any in negatives and questions. Both work with plural and uncountable nouns.

  • There are some eggs in the fridge.
  • We don’t have any milk.
  • Have you got any questions?

You might also see

  • Would you like some tea? / Can I have some water?Offers and requests use some, even though they’re questions.
  • Is there any milk left?

A lot of works with both and is the most natural choice in positive sentences. Much is mostly used in negatives and questions.

The basics

  • How many brothers and sisters have you got?
  • How much does this cost?
  • She has a lot of friends. / I don’t have much time.
  • I’ve got a few questions. / Add a little salt.

You might also see

  • There were too many people, so we left.
  • There isn’t enough sugar in this.
  • Few people know this. / We have little time.Without a, few and little are negative: not many, not much.
