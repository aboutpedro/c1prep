Grammar

Verb + -ing or to + verb

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

When one verb follows another, the second verb is either the -ing form (I enjoy swimming) or to + verb (I want to swim). It depends on the first verb, so it’s best to learn them in groups.

Verb + -ing or verb + to?

+ -ingenjoy, finish, don’t mind, can’t stand, stop, would you mind, go (for activities)
+ towant, would like, need, decide, hope, plan, learn, forget, promise
bothlike, love, hate, start: I like swimming. = I like to swim.

The basics

  • I enjoy listening to music.
  • Have you finished doing your homework?
  • I want to buy a new phone.
  • Would you like to come for dinner?

You might also see

  • Let’s go swimming. We went shopping.go + -ing for free-time activities
  • Would you mind closing the window?a polite way to ask
  • Don’t forget to call Grandma!

Careful: would like takes to, but like can take both: I’d like to go, not I’d like going.

-ing after prepositions

After a preposition (at, in, of, about, for, before, after, without…), a verb is always in the -ing form.

  • I’m not very good at drawing.
  • Are you interested in learning Japanese?
  • Thanks for helping me.
  • Wash your hands before eating.

You might also see

  • How about getting a pizza?a suggestion
  • He left without saying goodbye.

Spelling

  • make → making, dance → dancingdrop the e
  • swim → swimming, get → getting, shop → shoppingshort verbs ending in one vowel + one consonant: double it

Why? to + verb

To give the reason or purpose for something, use to + verb. With a noun, use for.

  • I went to the shop to buy some bread.
  • She’s saving money to buy a car.
  • We stopped for a coffee. = We stopped to have a coffee.

Careful: not for buy or for to buy. I went there to study, not for study.
