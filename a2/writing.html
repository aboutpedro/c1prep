Writing · Parts 6 and 7

Writing tasks

In Cambridge A2 Key you write a short email or note (Part 6) and a story about three pictures (Part 7). Your text stays in this browser as you type, so you can come back to it later.

How your writing is marked, and how to check it yourself

In A2 Key, your writing is marked on three things. Read your text again and ask yourself:

Content
Did I write about every point, or every picture? Will the reader understand everything?
Organisation
Is it one clear text, with linking words (and, but, because, so, then)? Does an email start and end like an email?
Language
Did I use the right tenses (the past simple for a story)? Is the spelling of everyday words right? Did I use some different words, not the same ones again and again?

Then compare your text with the model answer under each task. A different answer can be just as good: what matters is that it does the job well.

Part 6 · Email or note (25 words or more)

You want to go to the cinema with your English friend, Alex, on Saturday.

Write an email to Alex. In your email:

  • invite Alex to the cinema
  • say which film you want to see
  • say where you can meet.

Write 25 words or more.

Show a model answer

One good way to answer this task (45 words). Write your own answer first, then compare.

Hi Alex,

Would you like to come to the cinema with me on Saturday? There’s a new comedy about a family on holiday, and my sister says it’s really funny. It starts at 7.30. Let’s meet outside the cinema at 7.15.

See you soon,
Lucas

Why it works

  • All three points are there: the invitation, the film and where to meet.
  • Would you like to…? and Let’s… are simple, natural ways to invite and suggest.
  • It starts and ends like an email to a friend.

You have just moved to a new home.

Write an email to your English friend, Sam. In your email:

  • say where your new home is
  • say what you like about it
  • say when Sam can visit you.

Write 25 words or more.

Show a model answer

One good way to answer this task (41 words). Write your own answer first, then compare.

Hi Sam,

We’ve moved! Our new flat is in Green Street, near the park. I love my bedroom because it’s big and sunny, and I can see the trees from the window. Can you visit me next Saturday afternoon?

Love,
Bia

Why it works

  • It answers all three points: where the home is, what she likes and when Sam can come.
  • because gives a reason, which makes the email more interesting.
  • It ends with a question, so Sam knows he needs to reply.

You borrowed a book from your English friend, Jo, and you have lost it.

Write a note to Jo. In your note:

  • say sorry
  • explain what happened
  • say what you will do.

Write 25 words or more.

Show a model answer

One good way to answer this task (35 words). Write your own answer first, then compare.

Jo,

I’m really sorry, but I’ve lost your book. I took it on the bus on Monday, and I left it on the seat. I’ll buy you a new one this weekend.

Sorry again!
Leo

Why it works

  • It says sorry, explains what happened and says what Leo will do.
  • The past simple (took, left) tells the story; I’ll makes a promise.
  • A note can be short, but every point needs a full sentence.

You are going to have a birthday party.

Write an email to your English classmate, Chris. In your email:

  • invite Chris to the party
  • say when and where the party is
  • tell Chris what to bring.

Write 25 words or more.

Show a model answer

One good way to answer this task (40 words). Write your own answer first, then compare.

Hi Chris,

I’m having a birthday party, and I’d love you to come! It’s on Friday 12th May at 8 pm at my house, 24 Hill Road. Please bring some music, because we’re going to dance!

See you there,
Marta

Why it works

  • It gives all the information: the invitation, when, where and what to bring.
  • The present continuous (I’m having a party) is right for a plan.
  • Please bring…, because… asks for something and explains why.

You can't go to your English class tomorrow.

Write an email to your teacher, Ms Taylor. In your email:

  • say you can't come to the class
  • explain why
  • ask what homework you should do.

Write 25 words or more.

Show a model answer

One good way to answer this task (35 words). Write your own answer first, then compare.

Dear Ms Taylor,

I’m sorry, but I can’t come to the English class tomorrow. I have to go to the dentist at 10 o’clock. What homework should I do for next week?

Best wishes,
Paulo

Why it works

  • Dear Ms Taylor and Best wishes are polite ways to write to a teacher.
  • I have to… explains why he can’t come.
  • The question about homework is clear and polite.

Part 7 · Story (35 words or more)

Look at the three pictures. Write the story shown in the pictures. (In the exam you see three drawings; here each picture is described in words.)

  1. Picture 1: Max is walking his dog in the park. He throws a ball.
  2. Picture 2: The dog runs after the ball and jumps into a lake.
  3. Picture 3: Max goes into the water to help. Max and the dog are both wet, and people are laughing.

Write 35 words or more.

Show a model answer

One good way to answer this task (55 words). Write your own answer first, then compare.

Last Sunday, Max was walking his dog in the park. He threw a ball, and the dog ran after it. But the ball went into the lake, so the dog jumped into the water! Max went into the lake to help him. When they came out, they were both very wet, and everyone was laughing.

Why it works

  • It tells the story of all three pictures, in order.
  • The past continuous (was walking) sets the scene; the past simple tells what happened.
  • Linking words (and, but, so, when) join the sentences.

Look at the three pictures. Write the story shown in the pictures. (In the exam you see three drawings; here each picture is described in words.)

  1. Picture 1: A woman is waiting at a bus stop. It is raining.
  2. Picture 2: A car drives through a big puddle and the water goes all over her.
  3. Picture 3: The driver stops, says sorry and drives her to work.

Write 35 words or more.

Show a model answer

One good way to answer this task (53 words). Write your own answer first, then compare.

It was raining, and a woman was waiting at the bus stop. Suddenly, a car drove through a big puddle, and the water went all over her. She was very angry! But the driver stopped and said, “I’m so sorry!” Then he drove her to work, and they talked all the way there.

Why it works

  • was raining and was waiting describe the background; Suddenly starts the action.
  • It says how the woman felt (very angry).
  • The words of the driver make the story more alive.

Look at the three pictures. Write the story shown in the pictures. (In the exam you see three drawings; here each picture is described in words.)

  1. Picture 1: Two friends are making a cake in the kitchen.
  2. Picture 2: They are watching TV. There is smoke coming from the oven.
  3. Picture 3: The cake is black. They are eating pizza and laughing.

Write 35 words or more.

Show a model answer

One good way to answer this task (51 words). Write your own answer first, then compare.

One afternoon, Leo and Ana made a cake in the kitchen. They put it in the oven, and then they watched TV. After an hour, they saw smoke coming from the oven! They ran to the kitchen, but the cake was black. So they ordered a pizza and laughed about it.

Why it works

  • Time words (One afternoon, then, After an hour) make the order clear.
  • Every picture is in the story, with the funny ending.
  • It uses many past simple verbs correctly: made, put, saw, ran, ordered.

Look at the three pictures. Write the story shown in the pictures. (In the exam you see three drawings; here each picture is described in words.)

  1. Picture 1: A girl finds a wallet on the street.
  2. Picture 2: She takes it to the police station.
  3. Picture 3: The next day, an old man comes to her house with flowers to say thank you.

Write 35 words or more.

Show a model answer

One good way to answer this task (54 words). Write your own answer first, then compare.

Yesterday, a girl called Sofia found a wallet on the street. There was a lot of money in it, so she took it to the police station. The next day, an old man came to her house with some flowers. It was his wallet! He said, “Thank you so much!” Sofia was very happy.

Why it works

  • Yesterday and The next day show when things happened.
  • It adds small details (a lot of money) that are not in the pictures.
  • The end says how Sofia felt.

Look at the three pictures. Write the story shown in the pictures. (In the exam you see three drawings; here each picture is described in words.)

  1. Picture 1: A family is putting bags into their car. They are going on holiday.
  2. Picture 2: The car stops on the motorway. It won't start.
  3. Picture 3: The family is having a picnic next to the road while they wait for help.

Write 35 words or more.

Show a model answer

One good way to answer this task (54 words). Write your own answer first, then compare.

Last summer, the Silva family put their bags in the car because they were going on holiday. But on the motorway, the car stopped and it didn’t start again. They called a mechanic, and while they were waiting, they had a picnic next to the road. In the end, it was a fun day!

Why it works

  • because, but and while join ideas in longer sentences.
  • were going and were waiting (past continuous) explain what was happening.
  • In the end gives the story a good finish.
