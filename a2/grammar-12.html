Grammar

there is, there are; something, anyone, nowhere

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

We use there is / there are to say that something exists or is in a place: There’s a café on the corner. Words like something, anybody and nowhere talk about things, people and places when we don’t say exactly which.

The basics

  • There’s a supermarket near my house.
  • Are there any good restaurants here? – Yes, there are.
  • How many people are there in your family?

Careful: don’t use it has or have for this: There are two bathrooms in our flat, not It has two bathrooms or Have two bathrooms. In short answers, don’t shorten: Yes, there is.

Past and future

  • There was a big storm last night.
  • There were lots of people at the concert.
  • I think there will be rain tomorrow.

You might also see

  • There’s going to be a party on Saturday.
  • There might be a problem with the trains.
  • There used to be a cinema here.

-body and -one mean the same: somebody = someone, nobody = no one.

Use some- in positive sentences, any- in questions and negatives, no- with a positive verb to mean “not any”, and every- for all.

The basics

  • I want something to eat.
  • Is there anything good on TV?
  • I didn’t buy anything. = I bought nothing.
  • Everybody knows Leo.every- words take a singular verb: knows.

Careful: only one negative: I didn’t see anybody or I saw nobody, not I didn’t see nobody.
