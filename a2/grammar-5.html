Comparatives and superlatives

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

We use comparatives to compare two things (bigger, more expensive) and superlatives to say which one is at the top of a group (the biggest, the most expensive).

How to make them

adjectivecomparativesuperlative
short: cheapcheaperthe cheapest
ending in -e: nicenicerthe nicest
vowel + consonant: bigbiggerthe biggest
ending in -y: easyeasierthe easiest
long: expensivemore expensivethe most expensive
irregular: goodbetterthe best
irregular: badworsethe worst
irregular: farfurtherthe furthest

Comparatives: than

After a comparative, use than before the second thing.

The basics

  • My flat is smaller than yours.
  • This phone is more expensive than that one.
  • The weather is better today.

You might also see

  • It’s much cheaper online.Much, a lot and a bit make the difference bigger or smaller.
  • The film was less exciting than the book.
  • She’s older than me. / She’s older than I am.

Superlatives: the … in / of

Superlatives usually have the in front. After them we often use in with places and groups, and of with periods of time.

The basics

  • It’s the best restaurant in town.
  • Today is the hottest day of the year.
  • Who’s the youngest person in your family?

You might also see

  • She’s the most interesting person I’ve ever met.
  • That was the worst meal I’ve ever had.

as … as, too and enough

As … as says two things are the same. Not as … as means “less than”. Too means more than you want; enough means as much as you need. Enough goes after adjectives but before nouns.

  • Ben is as tall as his dad.
  • This hotel isn’t as nice as the last one.
  • The coffee is too hot to drink.
  • I’m not old enough to drive. / We don’t have enough chairs.

Careful: don’t mix the two forms: better, not more better; as big as, not as big than.
