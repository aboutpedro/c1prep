Relative clauses: who, which, that, where

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

A relative clause tells us which person, thing or place we mean: the woman who lives next door. It lets you join two short sentences into one.

Joining two sentences

whopeople: the man who fixed my car
whichthings and animals: the bus which goes to the airport
thatpeople or things: the book that I told you about
whereplaces: the town where I was born

Careful: the relative word replaces he, she, it, they or there, so don’t repeat them: a photo that I took, not a photo that I took it.

Using them

The basics

  • The woman who lives next door is a doctor.
  • I lost the watch which my grandfather gave me.
  • That’s the hospital where I was born.

You might also see

  • The pizza we ordered was cold.When there’s another subject after it (we), you can leave out that / which / who.
  • Everything that she said was true.after everything, something, nothing: that
  • The hotel which we stayed in was old. = The hotel where we stayed was old.

Careful: don’t use what after a noun: the book that I read, not the book what I read.

Describing things

Relative clauses are very useful when you don’t know a word: you can explain it.

  • a dentist = a person who looks after your teeth
  • a fridge = a machine that keeps food cold
  • a library = a place where you can borrow books
