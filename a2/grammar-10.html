Pronouns and possessives

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

Pronouns take the place of a noun, so we don’t have to repeat it: My brother lives in Canada. He works in a hotel. The form changes with its job in the sentence.

Subject or object?

The subject pronoun comes before the verb. The object pronoun comes after a verb or a preposition (for, with, to, near…).

The basics

  • She called me yesterday.
  • This present is for you.
  • Do you want to come with us?

You might also see

  • Lucy and I are in the same class.The subject: Lucy and I, not Lucy and me.
  • Who wants ice cream? – Me!In short answers we usually say me.

my or mine?

Use my, your, his, her, our, their with a noun. Use mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs without a noun.

  • This is my umbrella. → This umbrella is mine.
  • Is that your car or theirs?

Careful: its (no apostrophe) means “belonging to it”: The cat is eating its dinner. It’s means “it is”.

’s and s’

To say that something belongs to a person, add ’s. After a plural that ends in -s, add only ’.

one personmy sister’s bike · Leo’s phone
plural with -smy parents’ house · the boys’ room
plural without -sthe children’s toys · people’s names
two people, one thingTom and Ana’s house

You might also see

  • Whose phone is this? – It’s Leo’s.Whose asks who it belongs to. Who’s = who is.
  • I’m staying at my aunt’s this weekend.at someone’s = at their house.

myself, yourself…

Use a reflexive pronoun when the subject and the object are the same person: myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves.

  • Be careful! Don’t cut yourself.
  • We really enjoyed ourselves.enjoy yourself = have a good time
  • I made it myself.= nobody helped me
  • She lives by herself.by herself = alone

Careful: you is the same for one person and for more than one, but the reflexive changes: yourself (one), yourselves (more than one).
