The A2 Key exam

Checked against Cambridge English’s own pages on 26 November 2026

A2 Key shows that you can use English in simple, everyday situations: understand short notices and messages, write a short note, and talk about yourself. It has three papers. Its old name, KET, is still widely used.

Reading and Writing

1 hour · 50% of your overall score

Parts 1–5 are reading and Parts 6–7 writing; 32 questions in all.

PartTaskQuestionsPractice on this site
Part 1Multiple choiceSix short real-life texts, such as notices and messages: what does each one mean?6Practise
Part 2Multiple matchingThree short texts on the same topic and seven questions: which text says it?7Practise
Part 3Multiple choiceOne longer text with questions on detail and main ideas.5Practise
Part 4Multiple-choice clozeA short factual text with gaps: choose the right word for each.6Practise
Part 5Open clozeAn email (sometimes with its reply) with gaps: write one word in each.6Practise
Part 6Short email or noteWrite a short email or note. 25 words or more.1Practise
Part 7Picture storyWrite a story from three pictures. 35 words or more.1Practise

1 mark for each correct answer. Each writing task has up to 15 marks.

Listening

About 30 minutes, including 6 minutes to copy your answers · 25% of your overall score

You hear each recording twice; 25 questions.

PartTaskQuestions
Part 1Multiple choice with picturesFive short conversations: choose the picture that answers each question.5
Part 2Gap fillOne speaker gives information: complete the notes.5
Part 3Multiple choiceA longer conversation: three options for each question.5
Part 4Multiple choiceFive short recordings: what is each one mainly about?5
Part 5MatchingA conversation: match five people or things to the right information.5

1 mark for each correct answer.

Speaking

8–10 minutes for two candidates (13–15 for three) · 25% of your overall score

You take the test with another candidate and two examiners: one asks the questions, the other listens.

PartTaskTime
Part 1InterviewAnswer the examiner’s questions about yourself: where you live, your studies, your free time.3–4 minutes
Part 2DiscussionTalk with the other candidate about a topic: what you like or don’t like, and why.5–6 minutes

Results and the Cambridge English Scale

You get a score for each skill: reading, writing, listening and speaking. Your overall score is the average, so the Reading and Writing paper counts for half of it. All the scores are on the Cambridge English Scale, the same scale for every Cambridge exam (see all five exams on one scale).

ScoreResultCEFR level
140–150Grade AB1
133–139Grade BA2
120–132Grade CA2
100–119Level A1A1
80–99No certificate—

With 120 or more you get the A2 Key certificate, with your grade. With a score one level lower you get a certificate for that level. Scores from 80 to 99 appear on your Statement of Results, without a certificate.

On a computer or on paper

The format is the same on a computer and on paper. Paper-based A2 Key ends in 2026: from 2027, A2 Key is taken on a computer only (A2 Key Digital). A2 Key for Schools is still available on paper and on a computer.

A2 Key for Schools has the same format, with topics for school-age learners. Both exams lead to the same A2 Key certificate.
