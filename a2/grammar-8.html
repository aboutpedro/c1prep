Question forms

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

Most English questions follow the same pattern: an auxiliary verb (do, does, did, is, are, have, can, will…) comes before the subject. The auxiliary you need depends on the tense.

Yes/no questions

tensequestionshort answer
beAre you tired?Yes, I am. / No, I’m not.
present simpleDoes she live here?Yes, she does.
past simpleDid you enjoy it?No, I didn’t.
continuousIs it raining?Yes, it is.
present perfectHave you finished?No, I haven’t.
can / willCan you swim?Yes, I can.

Careful: after do, does and did, use the base verb: Does she live…?, not Does she lives…?

Wh- questions

Put the question word first, then the same order as a yes/no question: question word + auxiliary + subject + verb.

question wordauxiliarysubjectverb…

Useful question words: what, where, when, who, why, which, whose, how. How combines with lots of words: how much, how many, how often, how long, how old, how far. What does too: what time, what kind of.

The basics

  • What did you have for lunch?
  • How often do you go to the gym?
  • Whose bag is this?

You might also see

  • Who are you talking to? / Where are you from?In everyday English, the preposition goes at the end.
  • What’s the weather like?Asking someone to describe something.
  • Do you know where the station is?Inside another question, the order goes back to normal: not “where is the station”.

Who and what as the subject

When who or what asks about the subject (the person or thing that does the action), there’s no do / does / did. The verb stays in its normal form.

  • Who lives in that house? Not: Who does live…?
  • What happened? Not: What did happen?
  • Who do you live with? Here who is the object, so we need do.
