Present perfect

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

The present perfect connects the past with now. We use it for experiences in our life up to now, for recent news, and for situations that started in the past and are still true.

Form

have / has + past participle
+I’ve finished. She’s seen it.
−I haven’t finished. She hasn’t seen it.
?Have you finished? Has she seen it?

Regular past participles end in -ed, like the past simple: work → worked. Irregular ones need learning: be → been, do → done, see → seen, eat → eaten, write → written, go → gone, buy → bought, make → made.

Experiences: ever and never

When we talk about experiences, the exact time isn’t important, only that it happened at some point in our life.

The basics

  • I’ve been to Chile.
  • Have you ever eaten sushi? – No, I haven’t.
  • She’s never seen snow.

You might also see

  • This is the best pizza I’ve ever had.
  • It’s the first time I’ve driven a car.
  • She’s been to Paris. / She’s gone to Paris.Been: she went and came back. Gone: she’s there now.

Recent news: just, already, yet

Just means a very short time ago. Already means sooner than expected. Yet means “up to now” and goes at the end of negatives and questions. Just and already go before the past participle.

The basics

  • I’ve just finished my homework.
  • Have you done the shopping yet?
  • They haven’t arrived yet.
  • Don’t worry, I’ve already paid.

You might also see

  • Oh no! I’ve lost my keys.A past action with a result now: I can’t find them.
  • Look, it’s stopped raining.

How long? for and since

Use for with a period of time (for three days, for a long time) and since with the starting point (since Monday, since 2019, since I was a child).

  • We’ve known each other for ten years.
  • She’s worked here since January.
  • How long have you had that car?
  • I haven’t seen him for ages.

Present perfect or past simple?

If you say when something happened (yesterday, last year, in 2020, two days ago, when I was a child), use the past simple. A conversation often starts in the present perfect and then moves to the past simple for the details.

  • I’ve lost my keys. / I lost my keys yesterday.
  • Have you seen the new Batman film? – Yes, I saw it last weekend. It was great.

Careful: don’t use the present perfect with a finished time: I saw him yesterday, not I’ve seen him yesterday.
