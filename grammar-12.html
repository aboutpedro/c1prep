Linking words

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

Linking words show how ideas relate: contrast, reason, purpose, result. At C1, accuracy depends on what follows each linker – a clause, a noun or an -ing form – and on where it goes in the sentence.

Contrast and concession

linkersexample
+ a clause: although, even though, while, whereas, much asMuch as I admire him, I don’t trust him.
+ a noun or -ing: despite, in spite of, notwithstanding, for allDespite the rain / Despite being tired…
+ a clause: despite / in spite of the fact thatIn spite of the fact that he had no experience…
+ an adjective / adverb: albeitcomfortable, albeit expensive
+ a new sentence: however, nevertheless, nonetheless, even so, stillIt was risky. Even so, it worked.

Careful: Despite is never followed by of: despite the rain or in spite of the rain.

Reason, purpose and result

reason + nounowing to, due to, on account of, given: delayed owing to bad weather
reason + clausesince, as, seeing that: Seeing that you’re here…
purpose + verbso as (not) to, in order (not) to: She left early so as not to miss the bus.
purpose + clauseso that: He took notes so that he would remember.
precautionin case, for fear of + -ing: I’ll write it down in case I forget.
resultconsequently, as a result, hence + noun: The roads were icy, hence the delays.

Discourse markers

  • Far from being a failure, it was a huge success.
  • She’s a scientist, not to mention a talented musician.
  • We missed the train and, to make matters worse, it started to rain.
  • He’s talented. Be that as it may, he needs to work harder.formal = even so
  • I’m not going – at any rate, not today.
