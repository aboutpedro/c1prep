Relative and participle clauses

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

Relative and participle clauses let you pack more information into one sentence. They are typical of formal and written English, and of C1 reading texts.

Relative words

who / thatpeople: the woman who called
which / thatthings: the book that you lent me
whosepossession: the man whose car was stolen
whompeople, as an object, often after a preposition: the person to whom I spoke
where / when / whyplaces, times, reasons: the day when we met
wherebyby which (formal): a scheme whereby staff can buy shares

Careful: in non-defining clauses (between commas) use who or which, never that: The hotel, which was built in 1890, …

Quantifier + of whom / of which

  • She has three brothers, all of whom live abroad.
  • We saw several museums, none of which were open.
  • The house, the roof of which was damaged, is for sale.= whose roof
  • He failed the exam, which surprised everyone.which = the whole previous clause

Participle clauses

formmeaningexample
-ingactive, same timeAnyone wishing to leave… Not knowing what to do, I called him.
past participlepassiveThe paintings displayed here… Built in 1650, the castle…
having + past participleactive, completed firstHaving finished the report, she sent it.
having been + past participlepassive, completed firstHaving been warned, they stayed in port.

Careful: the subject of the participle clause must be the subject of the main clause. Walking home, the rain started suggests the rain was walking – say Walking home, I got caught in the rain.
