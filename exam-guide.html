Exam guide

The C1 Advanced exam

Checked against Cambridge English’s own pages on 26 November 2026

C1 Advanced shows that you can use English with confidence at university and at work: follow lectures and complex texts, write a well-argued essay or proposal, and take part in meetings and discussions. It has four papers. Its old name, CAE, is still widely used.

Reading and Use of English

1 hour 30 minutes · 40% of your overall score

56 questions on texts of about 3,000–3,500 words in all.

PartTaskQuestionsPractice on this site
Part 1Multiple-choice clozeA text with gaps: choose the right word for each (vocabulary).8Practise
Part 2Open clozeA text with gaps: write one word in each (mostly grammar).8Practise
Part 3Word formationA text with gaps: change the word given into the form that fits.8Practise
Part 4Key word transformationsRewrite a sentence with the word given so that it means the same.6Practise
Part 5Multiple choiceA long text with questions on detail, opinion, attitude and implication.6Practise
Part 6Cross-text multiple matchingFour writers on one topic: who agrees or disagrees with whom?4Practise
Part 7Gapped textSix paragraphs have been taken out of a text: put them back. One is extra.6Practise
Part 8Multiple matchingOne long text or several short ones: which part says what?10Practise

Parts 1–3 and 8: 1 mark for each correct answer. Part 4: up to 2 marks. Parts 5, 6 and 7: 2 marks.

Listening

About 40 minutes · 20% of your overall score

You hear each recording twice; 30 questions.

PartTaskQuestions
Part 1Multiple choiceThree short conversations, two questions on each.6
Part 2Sentence completionOne speaker for about 3 minutes: complete the sentences.8
Part 3Multiple choiceA conversation of about 4 minutes, with four options for each question.6
Part 4Multiple matchingFive short monologues on one theme and two tasks: match each speaker in both. On the computer, this part has been drag and drop since 1 November 2025: you drag each answer to a speaker.10

1 mark for each correct answer.

Speaking

15 minutes for two candidates (23 for three) · 20% of your overall score

You take the test with another candidate and two examiners: one asks the questions, the other listens.

PartTaskTime
Part 1InterviewAnswer the examiner’s questions about yourself.2 minutes
Part 2Long turnTalk about two of three pictures on your own for a minute, then answer a short question about your partner’s.1 minute each, plus 30 seconds
Part 3Collaborative taskDiscuss ideas with your partner for two minutes, then agree on a decision in a minute.3 minutes
Part 4DiscussionThe examiner asks you both wider questions on the topic of Part 3.5 minutes

Practise with a partner or a teacher: Speaking test 1 and Speaking test 2, each a full test with the examiner’s words, the pictures and a timer for each part.

Results and the Cambridge English Scale

You get five scores: Reading, Use of English, Writing, Listening and Speaking. Your overall score is their average, so the Reading and Use of English paper counts for two fifths of it. All the scores are on the Cambridge English Scale, the same scale for every Cambridge exam (see all five exams on one scale).

ScoreResultCEFR level
200–210Grade AC2
193–199Grade BC1
180–192Grade CC1
160–179Level B2B2
142–159No certificate—

With 180 or more you get the C1 Advanced certificate, with your grade. With a score one level lower you get a certificate for that level. Scores from 142 to 159 appear on your Statement of Results, without a certificate.
