Grammar

Reporting verbs

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

Instead of reporting with say and tell, C1 writers choose a verb that shows what the speaker was doing: promising, denying, accusing, warning. Each verb has its own pattern, and the pattern has to be learned with the verb.

Patterns

pattern and verbsexample
+ to-infinitive
agree, offer, promise, refuse, threaten		He threatened to call the police.
+ person + to-infinitive
advise, persuade, remind, urge, warn		She reminded me to lock the door.
+ -ing
admit, deny, recommend, suggest		She denied taking the documents.
+ preposition + -ing
apologise for, confess to, insist on, object to		He insisted on paying.
+ person + preposition + -ing
accuse of, blame for, congratulate on, prevent from, warn against		They accused him of lying.
+ that (+ should)
insist, recommend, suggest, demand		The doctor recommended that he (should) rest.

Careful: suggest is never followed by to or by a person + to: I suggest leaving / I suggest (that) we leave, not I suggest you to leave.

The subjunctive after that

After verbs and adjectives of demand or importance, formal English uses the base form for every person: no -s, no past.

  • The committee insisted that the report be published.
  • It is essential that every applicant submit the form by Friday.submits and should submit are also used

Reported questions

  • “Where have you been?” → She asked me where I had been.statement word order, no question mark
  • “Do you live here?” → He asked if / whether I lived there.
  • “Don’t touch anything.” → The guide told us not to touch anything.

Careful: times and places change when the situation changes: tomorrow → the next day, here → there, ago → before.
