Conditionals

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

At C1 conditionals stop being four neat types. Times can be mixed, if can disappear through inversion, and many other words can introduce a condition.

Mixed conditionals

conditionresultexample
past (had + past participle)present (would + verb)If I had accepted that job, I would be living in New York now.
present (past simple)past (would have + past participle)If she weren’t so stubborn, she would have apologised yesterday.

Inversion instead of if

In formal English, if can be dropped and the auxiliary moved to the front.

Had + subjectHad I known, I would have taken the train. = If I had known…
Should + subjectShould you need help, call us. = If you need…
Were + subject + toWere the government to raise taxes, there would be protests.
Were it not forWere it not for your support, I’d give up. = If it weren’t for…

Careful: in negative inversion, not comes after the subject: Had she not missed the flight… (not Hadn’t she missed…).

Other ways to express a condition

provided / providing (that), as long asonly if: You can go provided you’re back by six.
unlessif… not: Unless you leave now, you’ll miss the bus.
in caseas a precaution: Take an umbrella in case it rains.
supposing / suppose, assumingimagine: Supposing you won, what would you do?
but for + nounwithout: But for your help, we’d have failed.
otherwiseif not: Hurry, otherwise we’ll be late.
even if, whether… or notthe result doesn’t change: Even if you apologise, she won’t forgive you.
