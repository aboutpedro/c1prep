Future forms

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

English has no single future tense. The form you choose depends on whether you’re predicting, deciding, planning or describing a timetable, and on whether you’re looking at an action in progress or already finished at a future time.

Which future form?

willdecisions made now, offers, promises, predictions from opinion: I’ll get it. I think it will be fine.
going toplans decided before, predictions from evidence: We’re going to buy a car. Look at those clouds! It’s going to rain.
present continuousarrangements with a time and place: I’m seeing the dentist at three.
present simpletimetables and programmes: The train leaves at 7.15.

Careful: for a quick decision while you’re speaking, use will: ‘The phone’s ringing.’ ‘I’ll get it.’, not I’m going to get it.

Future continuous and future perfect

will be + -ingin progress at a future time: This time next week I’ll be lying on a beach.
will have + past participlefinished before a future time: I’ll have saved enough by June.

You might also see

  • Will you be using the car tomorrow?a polite way to ask about someone’s plans
  • Next June, they’ll have been married for thirty years.
  • By the time you get here, the film will have started.

Time clauses: no will

After when, as soon as, until, before, after, once, while, by the time, the moment, use a present tense for the future. The other part of the sentence takes the future form.

  • I’ll call you as soon as I arrive.not as soon as I will arrive
  • We won’t start until everyone gets here.
  • When you’ve finished, you can go.present perfect: the first action must be complete

Other ways to talk about the future

be about tovery soon: The concert is about to start.
be on the point of + -ingvery soon: I was on the point of leaving when…
be due toscheduled: The plane is due to land at 6.45.
be bound tocertain: You’re bound to pass.
be (un)likely toprobable / not probable: It’s likely to rain.
be set toexpected, ready (news): Prices are set to rise.
