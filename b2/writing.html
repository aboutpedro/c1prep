Writing · Parts 1 and 2

Writing tasks

In the exam you have 1 hour 20 minutes for both parts: one essay (Part 1) and one task you choose (Part 2). Write 140–190 words for each. Your text stays in this browser as you type, so you can come back to it later.

How your writing is marked, and how to check it yourself

In B2 First, your writing is marked on four things. Read your text again and ask yourself:

Content
Did I answer every part of the task, with relevant ideas? Would the reader be fully informed?
Communicative achievement
Is the register right for the reader and the type of text (formal for an application, lively for an article)? Does it do its job: persuade, inform, recommend?
Organisation
Are the paragraphs clear, each with one main idea? Do linking words and referencing (this, such, which) connect the ideas, without repeating the same ones?
Language
Did I use a range of vocabulary and structures (conditionals, passives, relative clauses) with good control? Are mistakes rare and minor?

Then compare your text with the model answer under each task. A different answer can be just as good: what matters is that it does the job well.

Part 1 · Essay (compulsory)

In your English class you have been talking about the lives of young people today. Now, your English teacher has asked you to write an essay.

Write an essay using all the notes and giving reasons for your point of view.

Do young people today have more opportunities than their parents had?
  • Notes — Write about:
  • 1. education
  • 2. travel
  • 3. ……………… (your own idea)
Show a model answer

One good way to answer this task (164 words). Write your own answer first, then compare.

It is often said that young people today have opportunities their parents could only dream of. In my opinion, this is largely true, although the picture is not entirely positive.

Firstly, education is far more accessible than it used to be. Thanks to the internet, anyone can take an online course or watch lectures from top universities for free. In addition, many more young people now go to university than in their parents’ generation.

Secondly, travel has become much cheaper and easier. Low-cost airlines and exchange programmes mean that many students spend time abroad, which was rare thirty years ago.

However, when it comes to work, the situation is less clear. Many young people have degrees but struggle to find a stable job, and housing is so expensive that they often live with their parents for longer.

In conclusion, I believe young people have more opportunities to learn and travel than their parents did, but they face new difficulties in building an independent life.

Why it works

  • The opinion is clear from the first paragraph and repeated, slightly developed, in the conclusion.
  • Each note has its own paragraph, and the third idea (work and housing) adds a contrasting point.
  • Linking words guide the reader: Firstly, In addition, Secondly, However, In conclusion.
  • The register is neutral: no contractions, and phrases like when it comes to and is far more accessible.

In your English class you have been talking about volunteering. Now, your English teacher has asked you to write an essay.

Write an essay using all the notes and giving reasons for your point of view.

Should all young people do some unpaid work to help their local community?
  • Notes — Write about:
  • 1. benefits for the community
  • 2. time for studying
  • 3. ……………… (your own idea)
Show a model answer

One good way to answer this task (150 words). Write your own answer first, then compare.

Volunteering is often presented as something every young person should do. While I agree that it is valuable, I do not think it should be compulsory.

There is no doubt that the community benefits. Volunteers help in hospitals, clean up parks and support elderly people who live alone. Without them, many local projects would simply not exist.

On the other hand, young people already have heavy workloads. Students preparing for important exams may not have time for regular unpaid work, and forcing them to do it could affect their results.

Another point is that volunteering only works well when people want to do it. Someone who is made to help may do the job badly, whereas a genuine volunteer brings energy and commitment.

In conclusion, I believe young people should be encouraged to volunteer, for example through school projects, but it should remain a free choice rather than an obligation.

Why it works

  • The answer is balanced (While I agree… I do not think…) but the opinion is never in doubt.
  • The writer’s own idea (people do better work when they choose to help) is relevant and well argued.
  • Good B2 language: There is no doubt that, would simply not exist, whereas, rather than.

In your English class you have been talking about work and study. Now, your English teacher has asked you to write an essay.

Write an essay using all the notes and giving reasons for your point of view.

Is it a good idea for students to have a part-time job while they are studying?
  • Notes — Write about:
  • 1. money
  • 2. experience
  • 3. ……………… (your own idea)
Show a model answer

One good way to answer this task (164 words). Write your own answer first, then compare.

Many students today combine their studies with a part-time job. In my view, this can be a good idea, as long as the job does not take up too much time.

The most obvious advantage is money. A part-time job allows students to pay for their own expenses, such as transport or books, and teaches them to manage a budget instead of relying entirely on their parents.

Work experience is another important benefit. Even a job in a café or a shop teaches skills like teamwork, punctuality and dealing with customers, all of which employers value. It can also help students decide what career they want.

However, a job can affect students’ studies. If they work late in the evenings, they may be too tired to concentrate in class or to prepare properly for exams.

To sum up, I believe a part-time job is worthwhile for students, provided that it is limited to a few hours a week and their studies remain the priority.

Why it works

  • The opinion includes a condition (as long as… / provided that…), which makes it more thoughtful.
  • Each paragraph starts with a clear topic sentence (The most obvious advantage is money).
  • all of which employers value is a good example of a relative clause that adds information neatly.

In your English class you have been talking about transport in towns and cities. Now, your English teacher has asked you to write an essay.

Write an essay using all the notes and giving reasons for your point of view.

Should public transport be free for everyone?
  • Notes — Write about:
  • 1. the environment
  • 2. the cost
  • 3. ……………… (your own idea)
Show a model answer

One good way to answer this task (158 words). Write your own answer first, then compare.

In many cities, traffic and pollution are serious problems. One solution that is often suggested is making public transport free for everyone. In my opinion, this would bring more advantages than disadvantages.

From an environmental point of view, the benefits are clear. If buses and trains were free, many people would leave their cars at home, which would reduce air pollution and carbon emissions.

The main argument against the idea is the cost. Someone has to pay for the service, and that would probably mean higher taxes. However, governments already spend large amounts on roads and on the health problems caused by pollution.

Free transport would also be fairer. People on low incomes, students and the elderly would be able to get to work, school or the doctor without worrying about the price of a ticket.

In conclusion, although free public transport would be expensive, I believe it would make our cities cleaner and fairer places to live.

Why it works

  • It deals with the argument against (the cost) and then answers it, which makes the opinion stronger.
  • The second conditional (If buses and trains were free, people would…) suits an imaginary situation.
  • The own idea (fairness) is different from both notes and clearly explained.

In your English class you have been talking about technology in everyday life. Now, your English teacher has asked you to write an essay.

Write an essay using all the notes and giving reasons for your point of view.

Do people depend too much on their mobile phones?
  • Notes — Write about:
  • 1. communication
  • 2. health
  • 3. ……………… (your own idea)
Show a model answer

One good way to answer this task (161 words). Write your own answer first, then compare.

Mobile phones have become part of almost every moment of our lives. In my opinion, many people do depend on them too much, even though they are extremely useful.

In terms of communication, phones have clear advantages. We can contact family and friends instantly, wherever they are. However, it is common to see friends sitting together in a café, each looking at their own screen instead of talking.

Health is another concern. Using a phone late at night can affect our sleep, and spending hours looking down at a small screen may cause neck and eye problems. Some people also feel anxious when they cannot check their messages.

Furthermore, we rely on phones for things we used to do ourselves. Few people remember phone numbers or can find their way around a city without a map app.

To conclude, I believe phones are valuable tools, but we should use them more consciously and make sure they do not control our lives.

Why it works

  • many people do depend on them uses do for emphasis, a nice touch at B2.
  • The communication paragraph looks at both sides before the writer’s point.
  • Specific examples (neck and eye problems, map apps) make the arguments convincing.

Part 2 · Choose one task

Article. You see this announcement in an English-language magazine:

Articles wanted: A day I will never forget

Tell us about a day that was special to you. What happened, and why do you still remember it so clearly? The best articles will be published in next month's magazine.

Write your article.

Show a model answer

One good way to answer this task (152 words). Write your own answer first, then compare.

The day I met my hero

Have you ever met someone you have admired for years? It happened to me last summer, and I still smile when I think about it.

I had queued for three hours in the rain outside a bookshop to get my favourite author to sign her new novel. By the time I reached the front, I was soaked and exhausted, and the staff were about to close the doors.

When she saw me, she laughed and asked how long I had been waiting. Then, instead of just signing the book, she talked to me for ten minutes about my own writing and told me never to give up.

I remember that day so clearly because it changed the way I see myself. Before, writing was just a hobby. Now I’m studying literature and working on my first novel.

Sometimes the best moments are worth getting wet for!

Why it works

  • The title and the opening question make the reader want to read on.
  • Narrative tenses are used well: had queued, was soaked, were about to close, had been waiting.
  • It answers both questions: what happened, and why the writer still remembers it.
  • The short, humorous final line is memorable.

Article. You see this notice on an English-language website:

My favourite place in town

Where do you most like to spend time in your town? What do you do there, and what makes it special? Write us an article and we will publish the most interesting ones.

Write your article.

Show a model answer

One good way to answer this task (154 words). Write your own answer first, then compare.

The best seat in town

If you’ve ever wanted to escape from the noise of the city for an hour, let me tell you about my favourite place: the rooftop garden of our central library.

Most people don’t even know it exists. You take the old lift to the sixth floor, walk past the history section, and suddenly you’re outside, surrounded by plants, wooden benches and an amazing view over the rooftops.

I go there almost every Saturday morning. I usually take a coffee and a novel, but sometimes I just sit and watch the city waking up below me.

What makes it special is the peace. Even though you’re right in the centre, all you can hear are birds and the wind in the trees. It’s the perfect place to think, read or simply do nothing.

So next time you’re in town, take the lift to the sixth floor. You won’t regret it!

Why it works

  • It talks directly to the reader (If you’ve ever wanted…, You take the old lift…), which suits a website.
  • The description is specific and visual: the old lift, wooden benches, the city waking up.
  • What makes it special is… is a useful structure for emphasis.

Email. You have received this email from your English-speaking friend, Alex:

I'm coming to your country for two weeks next summer and I'd love to visit you for a few days! What's the best thing to do in your area? What kind of clothes should I bring? Is there anything else I should know before I come?

Write your email.

Show a model answer

One good way to answer this task (152 words). Write your own answer first, then compare.

Hi Alex,

Great to hear you’re coming over next summer! You’re more than welcome to stay with us for a few days.

The best thing to do round here is definitely to go to the coast. It’s only an hour away by train, and there are some beautiful beaches where we could go surfing. I’ll also take you to the old town, which has great markets and restaurants.

As for clothes, it’s usually hot during the day, so bring shorts and T-shirts, but don’t forget a jumper, because it can get quite chilly in the evenings. You’ll need comfortable shoes too, as we’ll be walking a lot.

One more thing: shops here often close for a few hours after lunch, so don’t be surprised if the streets are empty in the afternoon. Also, it’s a good idea to bring some cash, as not everywhere takes cards.

Can’t wait to see you!
Mariana

Why it works

  • Informal but organised: one paragraph for each of Alex’s questions.
  • Natural informal language: round here, chilly, Can’t wait to see you!
  • Advice language: don’t forget, You’ll need, it’s a good idea to.

Letter. You see this advertisement in an English-language newspaper:

Summer camp assistants wanted

We need friendly, energetic people to help at our summer camp for children aged 8–12. You will help to organise sports, games and evening activities. Good English is essential. Write to us explaining why you would be suitable for the job.

Write your letter of application.

Show a model answer

One good way to answer this task (164 words). Write your own answer first, then compare.

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing to apply for the position of summer camp assistant, which I saw advertised in last week’s edition of your newspaper.

I am a nineteen-year-old university student, and I have a lot of experience of working with children. For the past two years, I have helped to run a football club for eight- to twelve-year-olds at my local sports centre, where I organise training sessions and games every Saturday.

I would describe myself as energetic, patient and creative. I enjoy planning activities, and I am good at making sure that every child feels included, even the shy ones. I also have a first-aid certificate.

As for my English, I have studied it for ten years and recently passed the B2 First exam, so I would have no difficulty communicating with the children or the other staff.

I would be happy to attend an interview at any time. I look forward to hearing from you.

Yours faithfully,
Ana Pereira

Why it works

  • The first paragraph says which job she is applying for and where she saw it.
  • Experience, personality and English each have a paragraph, with concrete examples.
  • Formal register throughout, and the right opening and closing (Dear Sir or Madam… Yours faithfully).

Report. Your English teacher has asked you to write a report about the sports facilities at your school or college. In your report, you should describe the facilities, say what students like about them and suggest how they could be improved.

Write your report.

Show a model answer

One good way to answer this task (167 words). Write your own answer first, then compare.

Report on the school’s sports facilities

Introduction
The aim of this report is to describe the sports facilities at our school, to say what students think of them and to suggest some improvements. It is based on a survey of sixty students.

Current facilities
The school has a large sports hall, an outdoor football pitch and a small gym with exercise bikes and weights. There are also changing rooms with showers.

What students like
Most students said that the sports hall is the best facility, as it is used for basketball, volleyball and badminton. The football pitch is also popular, especially at lunchtime.

Recommendations
However, many students complained that the gym is too small and that the equipment is old. It is therefore recommended that the gym be enlarged and new machines bought. In addition, the changing rooms need repainting, and the pitch would be used more if it had lights for winter evenings.

Conclusion
If these changes were made, more students would take part in sport.

Why it works

  • Clear headings make the report easy to read, and the introduction states its purpose.
  • Impersonal language: Most students said that…, It is therefore recommended that…
  • The recommendations are practical and linked to the problems students mentioned.

Report. The tourist office in your town wants to attract more young visitors. You have been asked to write a report describing what the town offers young people at the moment and recommending what could be done to make it more attractive to them.

Write your report.

Show a model answer

One good way to answer this task (163 words). Write your own answer first, then compare.

Attracting young visitors to Vila Nova

Introduction
This report describes what our town offers young people at the moment and suggests how it could become more attractive to them.

What the town offers now
Vila Nova has a beautiful old town with historic buildings and a popular weekly market. There is also a river beach, which is crowded with local teenagers in summer. However, there are few activities in the evenings, and most cafés close early.

Recommendations
Firstly, it would be a good idea to organise a summer music festival on the river beach, as this would bring in visitors from other towns. Secondly, the tourist office could produce a free map in English showing cheap places to eat and stay, such as the youth hostel. Finally, I would suggest hiring out bicycles, since the cycle paths along the river are one of the town’s best features.

Conclusion
With a few low-cost changes, Vila Nova could become a popular destination for young people.

Why it works

  • It describes the current situation honestly, including what is missing.
  • Each recommendation comes with a reason (as this would…, since…).
  • The conclusion is short and sums up the main message.

Review. You see this announcement on an English-language website:

Reviews wanted: A film everyone should see

Tell us about a film that you think everyone should watch. What is it about? Why did you like it so much? What kind of people would enjoy it most?

Write your review.

Show a model answer

One good way to answer this task (156 words). Write your own answer first, then compare.

A film that will stay with you

Every now and then, a film comes along that makes you look at the world differently. For me, that film is The Last Lighthouse.

It tells the story of Marta, a teenage girl who is sent to spend the summer with her grandfather, a lighthouse keeper on a remote island. At first she is bored and angry, but slowly she discovers a family secret that changes everything.

What I loved most was the acting. The young actress who plays Marta is completely convincing, and the scenes with her grandfather are both funny and deeply moving. The photography is stunning too: you can almost feel the wind and the sea.

Although it is a quiet film with very little action, it is never boring. Anyone who enjoys stories about families will love it, but I think teenagers in particular will relate to Marta.

Don’t miss it, and take some tissues!

Why it works

  • It describes the story briefly without giving away the ending (a family secret that changes everything).
  • Evaluative language: completely convincing, deeply moving, stunning.
  • It says who would enjoy it most and ends with a clear, lively recommendation.

Review. You see this notice in an English-language magazine:

Where to eat

Have you been to a café or restaurant recently? Write a review for our readers. Tell us about the food, the service and the atmosphere, and say whether you would recommend it.

Write your review.

Show a model answer

One good way to answer this task (148 words). Write your own answer first, then compare.

Café Lisboa: worth the wait?

Everyone in town has been talking about Café Lisboa, which opened on the main square last month, so last Saturday I finally went to see what all the fuss was about.

The food was definitely the highlight. I had a warm chicken salad with fresh herbs, followed by the best custard tart I have ever tasted. The portions were generous, and the prices were reasonable for the city centre.

Unfortunately, the service did not match the food. We waited almost twenty minutes before anyone took our order, and the waiter seemed rushed and forgot our drinks.

The atmosphere, on the other hand, was lovely. With its old tiles, soft music and big windows overlooking the square, it is a cosy place to spend an afternoon.

Overall, I would recommend Café Lisboa, especially for its cakes, but go early or be prepared to wait.

Why it works

  • Food, service and atmosphere each have their own paragraph, with specific details.
  • It balances positive and negative points (Unfortunately…, on the other hand…).
  • The recommendation is clear but honest about the problem.
