Relative clauses

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

Relative clauses add information about a noun. At B2 you need to tell defining and non-defining clauses apart, use prepositions with which and whom, and shorten clauses with -ing and past participles.

Relative words

who / thatpeople: the man who fixed my car
which / thatthings: the book which you lent me
whosepossession, + noun: the woman whose son won
whompeople as object, formal: the person to whom I spoke
where / when / whyplace, time, reason: the café where we met, the day when…, the reason why…
whatthe thing that, with no noun before it: What I need is a holiday.

Defining and non-defining

definingnon-defining
jobtells us which oneadds extra information
commasnoyes
thatyesno
leave out the pronoun?yes, if it’s the objectno
  • The film (that) we watched was terrible.defining: which film?
  • My sister, who lives in Canada, is coming.non-defining: I have one sister

Prepositions and which for a whole idea

  • the house in which I grew upformal; less formal: the house I grew up in
  • the colleague with whom I share an office
  • three brothers, all of whom live abroadalso some of / none of / most of / both of
  • He failed again, which didn’t surprise anyone.which = the whole idea before it

Careful: after a preposition use whom or which, never who or that: the person to whom I spoke.

Shortened relative clauses

-ing (active)The man standing by the door = who is standing
past participle (passive)The people invited to the party = who were invited
