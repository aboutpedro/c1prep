Grammar

Emphasis and inversion

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

English often changes the order of a sentence to put the focus on one part of it. These structures appear in B2 First, especially in key word transformations.

It is… that / who

  • It was my brother who broke the window.not me: the focus is on my brother
  • It wasn’t until midnight that we got home.
  • It was only when I got home that I realised.

What… is

  • What I need is a holiday.= The thing that I need is…
  • What we did was call the police.What + subject + did + was + verb
  • All I want is a cup of tea. The reason I’m late is that…

Inversion after negative words

When a negative or limiting word starts the sentence, the auxiliary comes before the subject, as in a question.

Never / Rarely / SeldomNever have I seen such a sunset.
Not only… but alsoNot only was he late, but he also forgot the tickets.
Hardly… when / No sooner… thanHardly had I sat down when the phone rang.
Only when / Not untilOnly when I saw it did I understand. (inversion in the main clause)
Under no circumstancesUnder no circumstances should you open this door.

Careful: with the past simple or present simple, add did, do or does: Rarely do you see…, Not until then did I understand.

do, so and such for emphasis

  • I do like your haircut! He did apologise in the end.do / does / did + verb
  • It was such a good film that we watched it twice.such (a) + adjective + noun
  • The exam was so difficult that nobody finished it.so + adjective; so many / so much + noun
