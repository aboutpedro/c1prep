Grammar

Verb patterns

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

When one verb follows another, the second verb can be -ing, to + verb, or the verb without to. It depends on the first verb, so it’s worth learning the patterns in groups.

-ing or to-infinitive?

+ -ingavoid, can’t help, can’t stand, consider, deny, enjoy, finish, imagine, keep, mind, miss, practise, risk, suggest: Would you mind closing the window?
+ toafford, agree, arrange, decide, expect, fail, hope, manage, offer, plan, pretend, promise, refuse, seem, tend: We managed to finish on time.
either, same meaningbegin, start, continue, like, love, hate, prefer: It started raining / to rain.

Careful: after a preposition, always use -ing: good at solving, without saying. In look forward to, be used to and object to, to is a preposition: I look forward to seeing you.

Verbs that change meaning

+ to+ -ing
remember / forgetto do a job: Remember to lock the door.a memory: I’ll never forget seeing it.
stopin order to: We stopped to have a coffee.no more: He stopped smoking.
trymake an effort: I tried to lift it.an experiment: Try restarting it.
regretsorry to give news: We regret to inform you…sorry about the past: I regret saying that.
meanintend: I meant to call you.involve: It means moving house.
go onchange activity: She went on to become a doctor.continue: He went on talking.

Verb + object + infinitive

+ person + toadvise, allow, ask, encourage, expect, persuade, remind, tell, want, would like: They want me to study law.
+ person + verb (no to)make, let: The film made me cry. Let me help.
helpwith or without to: Help me (to) carry these.

Careful: in the passive, make takes to: We were made to wait. Let has no passive: use be allowed to.

Expressions + -ing

  • It’s no use complaining.
  • There’s no point (in) waiting.
  • Is it worth visiting?
  • I have difficulty (in) understanding him.
  • I can’t help laughing.I can’t stop myself
