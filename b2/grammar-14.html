Quantifiers

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

Quantifiers say how much or how many. The right one depends on whether the noun is countable or uncountable, and whether the sentence is positive, negative or a question.

some, any, no, none

  • Would you like some coffee?some in positive sentences, offers and requests
  • There isn’t any milk. Have you got any questions?any in negatives, questions and after if
  • There’s no milk.no + noun = not any (with a positive verb)
  • "How many are left?" "None." None of the students passed.none alone or with of

How much and how many

countable (plural)uncountable
a large amountmany, a lot of, lots of, plenty ofmuch, a lot of, lots of, plenty of
some (positive)a few: a few friendsa little: a little milk
not much (negative)few: very few people camelittle: little hope

Careful: much and many are most common in negatives and questions. In positive sentences, a lot of sounds more natural: She’s got a lot of friends. And quite a few means quite a lot.

each, every, all, whole

  • Every student gets a certificate. Each team has eleven players.every / each + singular noun
  • All (of) the students passed.all + plural noun
  • I spent the whole day cleaning.the whole + singular noun = all of it
  • They cost €20 each. I swim every other day.

both, either, neither (two things)

  • Both my parents are teachers.the two of them
  • Sit at either end. I didn’t like either of them.one or the other; not… either
  • Neither of the hotels had rooms. Neither my brother nor my sister likes football.not one and not the other
