The passive and causative

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

Use the passive when the action matters more than the person who did it, or when you don’t know or don’t need to say who did it. At B2 you need it in every tense, after modals, in reporting structures and in have something done.

Forming the passive

be (in the right form) + past participle. Only the form of be changes.

tensepassive
present simpleEnglish is spoken here.
present continuousA new hospital is being built.
past simpleThe bridge was built in 1890.
past continuousMy car was being repaired.
present perfectA million copies have been sold.
past perfectThe money had been taken.
futureThe results will be published on Monday.
modalIt must be kept cold. It should have been finished.
-ing / infinitiveI hate being told what to do. She hopes to be chosen.
  • The novel was written by a student.by + the person who did it
  • The door was opened with a key.with + the thing used

Careful: verbs without an object (happen, arrive, die, go up, appear) can’t be passive: The accident happened, not was happened.

It is said that… / He is said to…

With verbs like say, think, believe, know, expect, report, claim, consider, there are two passive patterns. Both are common in news and formal writing.

It + passive + thatIt is said that the castle is haunted.
subject + passive + toThe castle is said to be haunted.

After to, choose the infinitive by time

same timeto + verb: She is known to speak six languages.
in progressto be + -ing: He is believed to be living abroad.
earlierto have + past participle: The fire is thought to have started in the kitchen.

have / get something done

have or get + object + past participle: someone else does the job for you, usually because you pay them.

  • I had my hair cut yesterday.a hairdresser cut it
  • We’re getting the kitchen painted next week.get is a little more informal
  • He had his phone stolen on the train.also for bad things that happen to you

Careful: the word order matters. I had my hair cut (someone cut it) is not the same as I had cut my hair (past perfect: I cut it myself).
