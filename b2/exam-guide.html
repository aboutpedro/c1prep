Exam guide

The B2 First exam

Checked against Cambridge English’s own pages on 26 November 2026

B2 First shows that you can live and work independently in English: follow the news, give and defend an opinion, and write clear texts on many subjects. It is Cambridge’s most popular exam. It has four papers. Its old name, FCE, is still widely used.

Reading and Use of English

1 hour 15 minutes · 40% of your overall score

52 questions on texts of about 2,200–2,500 words in all.

PartTaskQuestionsPractice on this site
Part 1Multiple-choice clozeA text with gaps: choose the right word for each (vocabulary).8Practise
Part 2Open clozeA text with gaps: write one word in each (mostly grammar).8Practise
Part 3Word formationA text with gaps: change the word given into the form that fits.8Practise
Part 4Key word transformationsRewrite a sentence with the word given so that it means the same.6Practise
Part 5Multiple choiceA long text with questions on detail, opinion and attitude.6Practise
Part 6Gapped textSix sentences have been taken out of a text: put them back. One is extra.6Practise
Part 7Multiple matchingOne long text or several short ones: which part says what?10Practise

Parts 1–3 and 7: 1 mark for each correct answer. Part 4: up to 2 marks. Parts 5 and 6: 2 marks.

Listening

About 40 minutes · 20% of your overall score

You hear each recording twice; 30 questions.

PartTaskQuestions
Part 1Multiple choiceEight short recordings on different subjects, one question each.8
Part 2Sentence completionOne speaker for 3–4 minutes: complete the sentences.10
Part 3Multiple matchingFive short monologues on one theme: match each speaker to one of eight statements. On the computer, this part has been drag and drop since 1 November 2025: you drag each answer to a speaker.5
Part 4Multiple choiceAn interview or conversation of 3–4 minutes.7

1 mark for each correct answer.

Speaking

14 minutes for two candidates (20 for three) · 20% of your overall score

You take the test with another candidate and two examiners: one asks the questions, the other listens.

PartTaskTime
Part 1InterviewAnswer the examiner’s questions about yourself.2 minutes

Part 3Collaborative taskDiscuss ideas with your partner for two minutes, then agree on one in a minute.3 minutes
Part 4DiscussionThe examiner asks you both wider questions on the topic of Part 3.4 minutes

Results and the Cambridge English Scale

You get five scores: Reading, Use of English, Writing, Listening and Speaking. Your overall score is their average, so the Reading and Use of English paper counts for two fifths of it. All the scores are on the Cambridge English Scale, the same scale for every Cambridge exam (see all five exams on one scale).

ScoreResultCEFR level
180–190Grade AC1
173–179Grade BB2
160–172Grade CB2
140–159Level B1B1
122–139No certificate—

With 160 or more you get the B2 First certificate, with your grade. With a score one level lower you get a certificate for that level. Scores from 122 to 139 appear on your Statement of Results, without a certificate.
