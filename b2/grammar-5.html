Wishes and regrets

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

To talk about things we’d like to be different, English moves the verb one step back into the past, just like in conditionals. The same idea works with would rather and it’s time.

wish and if only

about…formexample
nowwish + past simpleI wish I had a car. I wish it were warmer.
ability nowwish + couldI wish I could swim.
the pastwish + past perfectI wish I had studied harder.
someone’s annoying habitwish + wouldI wish you would stop interrupting!

If only works in the same way but sounds stronger: If only I’d listened to you!

Careful: don’t use wish + would about yourself or about states: I wish I were taller, not I wish I would be taller.

Other ways to express regret

  • You should have told me!should have + past participle: criticism of a past action
  • We shouldn’t have left the window open.
  • She regrets not going to university.regret + -ing (or having + past participle)

would rather, it’s time, had better

would rather + verbyour own preference: I’d rather stay in tonight. I’d rather walk than take the bus.
would rather + person + pastwhat you want someone else to do: I’d rather you didn’t tell anyone.
it’s (high) time + person + pastit should happen now, or should have already: It’s time we went home.
it’s time + to-infinitivewithout a person: It’s time to go.
had better (not) + verbstrong advice, or a warning: You’d better leave now. We’d better not wake the baby.

Careful: no to after would rather and had better: I’d rather stay, You’d better go.
