Imperatives, let’s and would like

Each set has 15 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

Here are three useful ways to tell people what to do, to make a suggestion, and to ask politely for something.

Instructions: Sit down. Don’t run.

Use the verb with no person before it. For the negative, use Don’t.

  • Open your books. Listen to me, please.
  • Be careful! Be quiet!
  • Don’t touch that! Don’t be late!

Ideas for you and me: Let’s…

  • I’m hungry. Let’s eat something.let’s + verb (no to)
  • It’s raining. Let’s not go out.negative: let’s not

would like

I’d like = I want (but more polite). Would you like…? = Do you want…?

  • I’d like a coffee, please.
  • Would you like some cake? – Yes, please. / No, thank you.
  • She’d like to go to Paris.would like + to + verb

Careful: Do you like tea? asks about tea in general. Would you like a tea? offers a tea now.
