Grammar

have got and has got

Each set has 15 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

We use have got to talk about family, things we own and how people look: I’ve got two sisters. She’s got long hair.

The forms

I / you / we / theyhe / she / it
+I’ve got a car.She’s got a car.
–I haven’t got a car.She hasn’t got a car.
?Have you got a car?Has she got a car?
short answersYes, I have. / No, I haven’t.Yes, she has. / No, she hasn’t.

The basics

  • We’ve got a new house. It’s got a big garden.
  • My brother hasn’t got a bike.
  • Have you got a pen? – No, I haven’t. Sorry.
  • What have you got in your bag?

Careful: she’s got = she has got. She’s happy = she is happy. The word got tells you which one it is.

You might also see

  • I have two sisters. Do you have a car?have without got means the same. It’s very common in American English.
  • I’ve got a cold. She’s got a headache.We also use have got for illnesses.
