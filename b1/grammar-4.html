The passive

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

In a passive sentence, the thing that the action happens to becomes the subject. We use it when the action or its result matters more than the person who did it, or when we don’t know who did it.

From active to passive

The object of the active sentence moves to the front. The verb becomes be + past participle. The person who did the action moves to the end after by, but we usually leave it out if it’s obvious, unknown or not important.

The basics

  • English is spoken in many countries.
  • This bridge was built in 1890.
  • My bike was stolen last night.
  • The Mona Lisa was painted by Leonardo da Vinci.Here who did it is interesting, so we keep by.

The passive in different tenses

Only be changes to show the tense. The past participle stays the same.

tensepassive
present simpleCoffee is grown in Brazil.
past simpleThe letters were sent yesterday.
present continuousThe road is being repaired.
present perfectOur flight has been cancelled.
will · going toThe results will be announced tomorrow.
modal verbsThis medicine must be kept in the fridge.

You might also see

  • I was born in 1995.Always passive in English.
  • The new stadium is going to be opened next year.
  • Smoking isn’t allowed in the building.
  • My phone got stolen on the train.In informal English, get sometimes replaces be.

Active or passive?

Use the active when you want to say who did something. Use the passive when the focus is on what happened.

  • My grandfather built this house. Focus on my grandfather.
  • This house was built in 1920. Focus on the house.

Careful: only verbs that have an object can be passive. Verbs like happen, arrive, die and appear can’t: The accident happened at night, not The accident was happened.
