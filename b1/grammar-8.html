Grammar

Reported speech

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

We use reported speech to tell someone what another person said, without quoting their exact words. Usually the tense moves one step back into the past, and words like I, here and tomorrow change to fit the new situation.

say or tell?

Tell needs a person after it. Say doesn’t, but if you add a person, use to. That is optional after both.

  • She said (that) she was tired.
  • She told me (that) she was tired.
  • He said to me that he was sorry.Not: He said me…

The tense moves back

direct speechreported speech
“I live in Rome.”She said she lived in Rome.
“I’m working.”He said he was working.
“I’ve finished.”She said she had finished.
“I saw it.”He said he had seen it.
“I’ll help.”She said she would help.
“I can swim.”He said he could swim.
“I’m going to leave.”She said she was going to leave.

Other words that change

today → that day · tomorrow → the next day · yesterday → the day before · now → then · here → there · this → that

  • “I’ll call you tomorrow.” → She said she’d call me the next day.
  • He says he’s tired.If the reporting verb is in the present, the tense doesn’t change.
  • She told me she lives in Rome.If it’s still true, you can keep the present.

Reported questions

Reported questions use the word order of a normal sentence: subject before verb, and no do, does or did. For yes/no questions, use if or whether. There’s no question mark.

  • “Where do you live?” → She asked me where I lived.Not: where did I live.
  • “Are you hungry?” → He asked if I was hungry.
  • “What time does the film start?” → I asked what time the film started.

You might also see

  • “What did you do at the weekend?” → He asked me what I had done at the weekend.
  • “Can you help me?” → She wanted to know whether I could help her.

Requests and orders

To report what someone asked or told you to do, use ask / tell + person + (not) to + verb.

  • “Please sit down.” → She asked us to sit down.
  • “Don’t touch it!” → He told me not to touch it.
