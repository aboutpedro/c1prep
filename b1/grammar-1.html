Narrative tenses

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

When we tell a story about the past, we mix tenses to show how events relate to each other in time. The past simple moves the story forward, the past continuous sets the scene, and the past perfect takes us further back.

The three narrative tenses together

tenseformjob in a story
past simplearrived, wentthe main events, one after another
past continuouswas / were + -ingthe background; an action in progress when something else happened
past perfecthad + past participlesomething that happened before another past event

Past perfect: further back in the past

Use the past perfect when you’re already talking about the past and need to go back to an earlier moment. It’s common with when, because, by the time, already, never and before.

The basics

  • When I got to the station, the train had left.It left first, then I arrived.
  • I didn’t recognise him because he had grown a beard.
  • By the time we arrived, everyone had gone home.

Compare: When I got to the station, the train left. Here the train left just as I arrived.

You might also see

  • It was the first time I had seen the sea.
  • She had never flown before, so she was quite nervous.
  • After he had finished dinner, he went out. / After he finished dinner, he went out.With after and before, the order is already clear, so the past simple is fine too.

Past continuous: setting the scene

  • It was raining and the streets were empty.
  • I was waiting for the bus when I saw an old friend.
  • While we were having lunch, the lights went out.

used to: how things were

Use used to + verb for past habits and states that are no longer true. In negatives and questions, it’s use to (no d), because did already shows the past.

  • I used to live in Porto, but now I live in Lisbon.
  • I didn’t use to like olives. Now I love them.
  • Did you use to play any sports at school?

Careful: used to is for things that happened regularly or were true for a long time, not single events. I went to Paris in 2015, not I used to go to Paris in 2015.
