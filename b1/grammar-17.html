Grammar

should have, could have, must have

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

A modal + have + past participle lets you look back at the past: to say what was a good idea, what was possible, or how sure you are about what happened. I should have left earlier. She must have forgotten.

The form

modalshould, could, might, may, must, can’t
+ havealways have, never has or had
+ past participleleft, gone, been, seen, forgotten

Careful: in speech, have sounds like “of”: should’ve. But we never write should of.

Regrets and criticism: should have

  • I failed. I should have studied harder.It was a good idea, but I didn’t do it.
  • I feel sick. I shouldn’t have eaten so much.I did it, but it was a bad idea.
  • You should have told me you were coming!

Possible, but it didn’t happen: could have

  • You could have taken a taxi. Why did you walk?
  • That was dangerous! You could have been killed.
  • She could have been a singer, but she became a teacher.

Guessing about the past

  • The streets are wet. It must have rained.I’m sure it did.
  • She isn’t answering. She might have left her phone at home.maybe (may have / could have are similar)
  • That can’t have been Tom. He’s in Australia.I’m sure it didn’t happen (couldn’t have is similar)

Careful: the opposite of must have here is can’t have, not mustn’t have.
