Grammar

-ing or to-infinitive?

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

When one verb follows another, the second verb is either an -ing form (I enjoy swimming) or a to-infinitive (I want to swim). It depends on the first verb, so it’s worth learning the most common ones in groups.

Verb + -ing or verb + to?

+ -ing+ to-infinitive
enjoy, finish, mind, avoid, suggest, consider, keep, practise, miss, imagine, can’t standwant, would like, decide, hope, plan, promise, agree, refuse, manage, offer, learn, afford, seem, expect

The basics

  • I enjoy cooking for friends.
  • Have you finished reading that book?
  • We decided to stay at home.
  • She promised to call me.

You might also see

  • Would you mind closing the window?A polite request.
  • My parents want me to study medicine.Want, ask, tell and expect can have an object before to.
  • I started learning / to learn Spanish last year.Like, love, hate, start, begin and continue can take either, with little difference.

After prepositions, after adjectives, and as the subject

After a preposition, always use -ing. After many adjectives, use to. When a verb is the subject of a sentence, use -ing.

  • I’m interested in learning Japanese.
  • Thanks for helping me. / Wash your hands before eating.
  • It’s easy to understand. / I was surprised to see him.
  • Swimming is good for your back.

You might also see

  • I’m looking forward to seeing you.Here to is a preposition, so it’s followed by -ing.
  • It’s not worth waiting. The bus has gone.

Purpose: to or for?

To say why you do something, use to + verb. Use for + noun.

  • I went to the shop to buy some milk. Not: for buy / for to buy.
  • Let’s go out for a coffee.

Verbs that change their meaning

With remember, forget, stop and try, the -ing form and the to-infinitive mean different things.

+ to+ -ing
remember · forgeta job to do: Don’t forget to call your gran.a memory: I’ll never forget meeting her.
stopstop in order to do something: We stopped to have lunch.not do it any more: He stopped smoking.
trymake an effort: I tried to open it, but I couldn’t.do it as an experiment: Try restarting your computer.
