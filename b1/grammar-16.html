Grammar

have something done

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

When someone does a job for you, usually because you pay them, we use have something done: I had my hair cut. (The hairdresser cut it, not me.)

The form

havein any tense: have, had, am having, will have, have had…
+ objectmy hair, the car, our kitchen
+ past participlecut, washed, painted, repaired

The basics

  • I had my car serviced last week.
  • She has her nails done every two weeks.
  • We’re having a new kitchen fitted next month.

You might also see

  • You should have that cough checked.
  • Where did you have your hair cut?
  • We’ve just had the house painted.

Careful: the word order changes the meaning. I had my hair cut = someone cut it for me. I had cut my hair = past perfect: I did it myself, before another moment in the past.

get something done

In informal English, get often replaces have. The meaning is the same.

  • I must get my hair cut.
  • Where did you get your phone fixed?

Bad experiences

The same structure is used for bad things that happen to you, when someone else does them. Here, nobody asked or paid for it!

  • He had his wallet stolen on the train.
  • We had our flight cancelled because of the storm.
  • They had their windows broken in the storm.
