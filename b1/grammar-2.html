Present perfect simple and continuous

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

Both forms connect the past with now. The present perfect simple (have done) focuses on the result or on how many. The present perfect continuous (have been doing) focuses on the activity and how long it has been going on.

Form

simplecontinuous
+I’ve paintedI’ve been painting
−She hasn’t paintedShe hasn’t been painting
?Have you painted…?Have you been painting…?

Activity or result?

Use the continuous for an activity that started in the past and is still going on, or has only just stopped. Use the simple for what has been completed, especially when you say how many or how much.

The basics

  • I’ve been waiting for twenty minutes.And I’m still waiting.
  • You look tired. Have you been running?It’s just stopped, and you can see the result.
  • I’ve written three emails this morning.How many: simple.

You might also see

  • How long have you been learning English? / How many lessons have you had?
  • It’s been raining all day.
  • She’s been working here since she left university.After since, use the past simple for the starting point.

Careful: state verbs like know, be, have (own), like and understand stay in the simple form: I’ve known her for years, not I’ve been knowing her.

Present perfect or past simple?

As soon as you mention a finished time (yesterday, in 2018, last week, two years ago), switch to the past simple.

  • I’ve been to Japan twice. I went there in 2018 and 2022.
  • He’s worked here for five years. Before that, he worked in a hotel.
