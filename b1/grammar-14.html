Grammar

Comparing; so, such, too and enough

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

At B1 you can say how big a difference is (much cheaper, a bit slower), say that two things are equal or not (as … as), and use so, such, too and enough to say how much of a quality something has.

How big is the difference?

bigmuch / far / a lot + comparative: much cheaper, far better, a lot more expensive
smalla bit / a little / slightly + comparative: a bit slower, slightly bigger

Careful: not very with a comparative: much better, not very better.

You might also see

  • It’s even colder today.Yesterday was cold, and today is colder still.
  • The more you practise, the better you get.Two things change together.
  • It’s getting colder and colder.a continuing change

The same or different?

  • Ana is as tall as Leo.the same
  • The bus isn’t as fast as the train.= the bus is slower
  • My phone is the same as yours. Your bag is similar to mine.
  • Life in the city is different from life in the country.

You might also see

  • He earns as much money as his boss. There weren’t as many people as we expected.
  • Come as soon as you can.

so or such?

so+ adjective or adverb: so tired, so fast, so many, so much
such+ (a / an) + (adjective) + noun: such a good time, such nice people, such beautiful weather
  • The film was so boring that I fell asleep. = It was such a boring film that I fell asleep.

too and enough

  • This coffee is too hot to drink.too + adjective: more than is good
  • He isn’t old enough to drive.adjective + enough
  • There isn’t enough room for everyone.enough + noun
  • There were too many people. I drank too much coffee.

Careful: enough goes after an adjective but before a noun: tall enough, but enough time.
