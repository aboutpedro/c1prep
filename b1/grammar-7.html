Grammar

used to, be used to and get used to

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

These three look almost the same, but they mean different things. used to is about the past. be used to and get used to are about something being normal, or becoming normal, for you.

Three meanings

used to + verba past habit or state that isn’t true now: I used to live in Rome.
be used to + -ingsomething is familiar, not strange: I’m used to living here.
get used to + -ingsomething is becoming familiar: I’m getting used to driving on the left.

After be used to and get used to, you can also use a noun or pronoun: the noise, it, my new job.

used to + verb: the past

Use it for things that happened regularly in the past, or were true for a long time, but have stopped. In negatives and questions, write use to.

The basics

  • I used to play the piano when I was a kid.
  • There used to be a cinema here. Now it’s a supermarket.
  • She didn’t use to like coffee.
  • Did you use to have long hair?

You might also see

  • Every summer, we would drive to the coast.Would can also describe past habits, but not past states: not “There would be a cinema here”.
  • I never used to eat breakfast.

Careful: there’s no present form. For present habits, use the present simple: I usually walk to work, not I use to walk to work.

be used to: it’s normal for me

Here, used works like an adjective meaning “familiar with”. The verb be can be in any tense.

The basics

  • I’m used to getting up early. I’ve done it for years.
  • She isn’t used to the cold. She’s from Brazil.

You might also see

  • At first the job was hard because I wasn’t used to working with children.
  • Are you used to your new glasses yet?

get used to: it’s becoming normal

This describes the change, the process of something new becoming normal. Again, get can be in any tense.

The basics

  • I’m getting used to my new job.
  • Don’t worry, you’ll soon get used to it.

You might also see

  • It took me months to get used to driving on the left.
  • I can’t get used to eating dinner so late.

Careful: after be used to and get used to, use -ing, not the base verb. Here to is a preposition: I’m used to working, not I’m used to work.
