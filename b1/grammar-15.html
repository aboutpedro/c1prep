Grammar

Linking words

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

Linking words show how ideas connect: contrast, reason, result, purpose or addition. The key at B1 is knowing what comes after them: a full clause (subject + verb), a noun, or an -ing form.

What comes after?

+ subject + verb+ noun / -ing
contrastalthough, even though, butdespite, in spite of
reasonbecause, as, sincebecause of, due to
purposeso thatto / in order to + verb, for + noun
  • Although it was raining, we went out. = Despite the rain, we went out.
  • The match was cancelled because it rained / because of the rain.
  • I’m saving to buy a car. I’m saving so that I can buy a car.

Contrast

The basics

  • It was cold, but we went swimming.
  • Although it was cold, we went swimming.
  • It was cold. However, we went swimming.However usually starts a new sentence, with a comma.

You might also see

  • Despite being tired, she finished her homework.
  • Despite the fact that it was expensive, we bought it.a way to use despite with a full clause
  • My kids love it, though.In conversation, though can go at the end.
  • I like cats, whereas my sister prefers dogs.compares two different facts

Careful: despite has no of; in spite of always has it. And never despite of.

Reason and result

  • I didn’t go out because I had homework.
  • As it’s your birthday, I’ll pay.as / since = because, often at the start
  • I was tired, so I went to bed. I missed the bus. That’s why I was late.

Purpose and addition

  • We left early in order to avoid the traffic.more formal than to
  • She whispered so as not to wake the baby.
  • The hotel was cheap. It was also comfortable. / It was comfortable as well.
  • The job is well paid. In addition, the people are nice.
  • As well as singing, she writes her own songs.

Careful: in negative sentences, use either: I don’t eat meat, and my brother doesn’t either.
