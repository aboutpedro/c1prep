Modal verbs

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

Modal verbs let us talk about what’s necessary, what’s a good idea, what’s possible, and how sure we are about something. Like all modals, they’re followed by the base verb, without to (except have to and ought to).

Obligation and necessity

mustnecessary: the speaker feels it’s important, or it’s a written rule. I must call my mum tonight.
have tonecessary: a rule or a situation from outside. I have to wear a tie at work.
mustn’tnot allowed. You mustn’t use your phone in the exam.
don’t have to
don’t need to
needn’t		not necessary. You don’t have to come if you’re busy.

You might also see

  • We had to wait for two hours.Must has no past form, so we use had to.
  • You needn’t take your shoes off.
  • Will I have to pay extra?

Advice: should, ought to, had better

Should and ought to mean the same, but should is much more common. Had better (’d better) is stronger: it suggests something bad will happen if you don’t.

  • You should get more sleep.
  • You ought to apologise to her.
  • You’d better leave now or you’ll miss the train.
  • We’d better not be late again.The negative is had better not, not hadn’t better.

Possibility: may, might, could

Use may, might or could when something is possible but you’re not sure. Might is the most common in conversation.

  • It might rain later. Take a coat.
  • She may be at work, I’m not sure.
  • Don’t call now. He could be asleep.
  • I might not come to the party.For “possibly not”, use might not or may not. Couldn’t means “impossible”.

How sure are you? must, might, can’t

When we draw a conclusion from what we know, we use must if we’re sure something is true, can’t if we’re sure it isn’t, and might, may or could if it’s possible.

The basics

  • The lights are on. She must be at home.
  • Her car isn’t here. She can’t be at home.
  • I’m not sure where she is. She might be at the gym.

You might also see

  • You must be joking!
  • Don’t make a noise. The baby might be sleeping.Modal + be + -ing for something happening now.
  • She’s not answering. She must have left already.Modal + have + past participle for conclusions about the past.

Careful: the opposite of must be here is can’t be, not mustn’t be: That can’t be Tom. He’s in Paris.
