Grammar

Question tags and indirect questions

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

Question tags (…, isn’t it?) and indirect questions (Could you tell me…?) make you sound friendlier and more polite. Both follow simple rules about auxiliary verbs and word order.

Question tags

A question tag is a short question at the end of a sentence. We use it to check something or to invite the other person to agree. Use the same auxiliary as the sentence, and the opposite: positive sentence → negative tag, negative sentence → positive tag.

beYou’re from Brazil, aren’t you? · It isn’t far, is it?
can, will…She can drive, can’t she? · They won’t be late, will they?
haveYou’ve seen it, haven’t you?
do, does, didYou live here, don’t you? · She works in a bank, doesn’t she? · They left, didn’t they?

Careful: your voice matters. If your voice goes down on the tag, you’re sure and want agreement. If it goes up, it’s a real question.

Indirect questions

Start with a polite phrase like Could you tell me…? Do you know…? Can I ask…? I wonder… Then use normal word order: subject + verb, with no do, does or did.

The basics

  • Where is the station? → Could you tell me where the station is?
  • What time does the bank open? → Do you know what time the bank opens?
  • Where did you buy it? → Can I ask where you bought it?

Yes / no questions: if or whether

  • Is the museum open? → Do you know if the museum is open?
  • Can I park here? → Could you tell me whether I can park here?
