Future forms

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

English has no single future tense. The form you choose shows how you see the future: a decision you’re making now, a plan, an arrangement in your diary, or a timetable.

The main forms

willdecisions made now, offers, promises, predictions with I think
I’m cold. – I’ll close the window.
going toplans you already decided, predictions from what you can see
We’re going to buy a car. Look at those clouds! It’s going to rain.
present continuousfixed arrangements, usually with a time or place
I’m seeing the dentist at 4 tomorrow.
present simpletimetables and programmes
The train leaves at 6.15.

You might also see

  • What are you doing this weekend?the most natural way to ask about plans
  • I promise I won’t tell anyone.
  • Shall I carry that for you?offers and suggestions with I and we

Careful: often more than one form is correct. The difference is in how you see it: I’m going to see Ana (my plan) and I’m seeing Ana at 6 (we’ve arranged it) are both fine.

After when, as soon as, until…

After time words like when, as soon as, until, before and after, use the present, not will.

  • I’ll text you as soon as I arrive.
  • Don’t start until I tell you.
  • I’m going on holiday after I finish my exams.

will be doing, will have done

Will be + -ing (future continuous) is for something in progress at a time in the future. Will have + past participle (future perfect) is for something finished before a time in the future, often with by.

The basics

  • Don’t call at 8. We’ll be having dinner.
  • This time next week, I’ll be lying on a beach.
  • I’ll have finished the book by Friday.
  • By 2030, they will have built the new airport.

You might also see

  • By next June, I’ll have worked here for five years.
  • If we leave at 8.15, the film will have started.
