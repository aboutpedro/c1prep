Quantifiers

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

Quantifiers tell us how many or how much: both, all, every, a few, none. Some go with countable nouns, some with uncountable nouns, and some change meaning with one small word: few and a few are not the same.

both, either, neither: talking about two

  • Both my brothers live in Canada. / Both of them live there.
  • You can take either bus.either + singular noun: one or the other
  • Neither of my parents speaks English.
  • I like both pizza and pasta. We can go either today or tomorrow. Neither Tom nor Ana came.

Careful: with a negative verb, use either, not neither: I didn’t buy either of them = I bought neither of them.

all, every, each

all+ plural or uncountable: all students, all the cake, all day
every+ singular, the whole group: every morning, almost every house, every ten minutes
each+ singular, one by one, often for a small group or two: in each hand, twenty euros each

You might also see

  • All of my friends came. · Each of the children got a present.Not every of: use every one of.
  • I waited all day. ≠ I wait every day.all day = the whole day; every day = on each day

few, a few, little, a little

a few / a littlesome (positive): a few friends, a little milk
few / littlenot many / not much (negative): few people, little time

Few goes with countable nouns, little with uncountable nouns.

  • I have a few friends in London, so I’m not lonely.
  • Very few people came. The room was almost empty.
  • We have little time, so hurry up!

no, none, any, most

  • There’s no milk. = There isn’t any milk.no + noun
  • How many are left? – None. / None of my friends could come.none without a noun, or none of
  • Sit in any seat you like.any = it doesn’t matter which
  • Most people like summer. · Most of the students are from Brazil.most + noun in general; most of + the / my / these…
