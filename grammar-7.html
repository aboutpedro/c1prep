Modal verbs

Each set has 10 sentences. Follow the instructions above the sentences, then press Check answers.

Modal verbs let us say how sure we are and how we judge an action. At C1 the key is the past: modal + have + past participle for guesses about the past and for criticism, regret and unnecessary actions.

Deduction: how sure are you?

presentpast
sure it’s trueShe must be exhausted.They must have gone out.
possibleIt might / may / could be Tom.I might have left them at work.
sure it’s not trueThat can’t be Julia.He can’t / couldn’t have received it.

Careful: the opposite of must in deduction is can’t, not mustn’t. For an activity, use the continuous: She must have been waiting for hours.

Criticism, regret and unnecessary actions

should / ought to haveit was the right thing, but it didn’t happen: You should have told me.
could / might havea possibility that didn’t happen, or annoyance: You could have warned us!
would havewhat someone wanted to do: He would have come, but he was ill.
needn’t haveit happened, but wasn’t necessary: We needn’t have rushed.
didn’t need toit wasn’t necessary (usually it didn’t happen): I didn’t need to buy milk.

Expressions with a modal meaning

be bound tocertain: We’re bound to be late.
be (un)likely toprobable / improbable: It’s unlikely to be ready.
be supposed toa rule, or what was expected: He was supposed to arrive at nine.
may / might wellquite likely: She may well be right.
might as wellthere’s no reason not to: You might as well go home.
wouldn’trefused to: The car wouldn’t start.
could hardlywas almost unable to: I could hardly believe it.

Careful: for one success in the past, use was able to / managed to, not could: The rescuers were able to reach them.
